# Slice 04 — Kid movement and failure

Who may move, who must stop, and what a mistake does. Player-facing only. The words below are the two games and the merge rule. No score, points, stars, lives, or ranks are added.

## 1. Scope

In scope:

- Where the kids are when a phase’s movement starts.
- The motion each beat requires, who does it, and who that beat leaves out of that motion.
- A mistake’s result when the beat states one.

Out of scope except as a handoff line: how the teacher changes state, what the props look like beyond the choice a mover must use, note and doodle artwork, and both victory celebrations.

A failed beat sends the kids back only by that beat’s own rule. Beats with no failure rule stay UNKNOWN. This slice does not invent a send-back, a timer, or a point penalty for them.

## 2. Requirements

### Shared order

**REQ-04-001.** Movement rules apply in the written order: Phase A beats 1, 2, 3, 4, then 5, then Phase B beats 6, 7, 8, 9, then 10. A beat’s moving and stopping rule is the rule written for that beat.

> Merge rule. “Phase A is Game 1, beats 1–5, in the order written.” “Beats 6–10 run in the order written.”

**REQ-04-002.** The kids move through the same room in Phase B as in Phase A. Props already in the room stay in the room.

> Merge rule. “Props and the room carry from Phase A into Phase B.”

**REQ-04-003.** When a beat fails, the kids are sent back by the rule that beat states. No other send-back is substituted.

> Merge rule. “A failed beat sends the kids back as that beat’s own rule says.”

### Phase A — approach from the back

**REQ-04-004.** Phase A starts with the kids at the back of the room. They sneak toward the teacher, who is sleeping at the desk at the front.

> Game 1. “The kids must sneak up from the back of the room.” “The teacher is sleeping at their desk at the front of the room.”

**REQ-04-005.** If the teacher wakes up, the kids must immediately stop and freeze in a ridiculous, funny pose.

> Game 1. “If the teacher wakes up, the kids must immediately freeze in a ridiculous, funny pose.”

**REQ-04-006.** On beat 1, the kids walk while holding either a squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap under their feet. Both items are allowed choices.

> Game 1 beat 1. “Kids must walk across the room while holding a squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap under their feet.”

**REQ-04-007.** On beat 1, a silent sneak is impossible. The walk sounds hilarious.

> Game 1 beat 1. “Sneaking silently is impossible and sounds hilarious.”

**REQ-04-008.** Beats 2, 3, 4, and 5 still happen after beat 1, in that order. Finishing beat 1 is not by itself the desk claim in beat 5.

> Merge rule. Beats 1–5 stay in order, and none of the ten beats is dropped. Game 1 beats 4 and 5 still place a checkpoint and the desk after beat 1.

**REQ-04-009.** If the teacher has been made to laugh uncontrollably for 5 seconds, everyone has those 5 seconds to move.

> Game 1 beat 2. “start laughing uncontrollably for 5 seconds, giving everyone time to move.”

**REQ-04-010.** When the teacher suddenly opens their eyes, anyone caught moving must instantly stop and freeze, imitating a goofy statue.

> Game 1 beat 3. “When the teacher suddenly opens their eyes, anyone caught moving must instantly freeze and imitate a goofy statue.”

**REQ-04-011.** The goofy statue the source illustrates is balancing on one foot with a finger on the nose.

> Game 1 beat 3. “like balancing on one foot with a finger on their nose.”

**REQ-04-012.** The wake freeze in REQ-04-005 and the caught-moving statue in REQ-04-010 both stay in force. They are not rewritten as a single rule.

> Game 1, and Game 1 beat 3. The intro names the kids and a ridiculous, funny pose. Beat 3 names anyone caught moving and a goofy statue.

**REQ-04-013.** If they laugh at the copycat freeze, they have to take three steps back.

> Game 1 beat 3. “If they laugh, they have to take three steps back.”

**REQ-04-014.** Three steps back is the only stated send-back for laughing at that freeze. The beat does not add a further penalty on top of those three steps.

> Game 1 beat 3, and the merge rule. The beat’s own rule is the three steps. Nothing further is written.

**REQ-04-015.** To pass the mid-way checkpoint line, a kid must sneak up to the teacher and stick on the funny sticky note without the teacher noticing.

> Game 1 beat 4. “To pass a mid-way checkpoint line, a kid must successfully sneak up and stick a funny sticky note … onto the teacher’s back without the teacher noticing.”

**REQ-04-016.** Beat 5’s successful approach is a kid reaching the desk without getting caught.

> Game 1 beat 5. “When a kid successfully reaches the desk without getting caught.”

### Phase B — across the room

**REQ-04-017.** MERGE-CHOICE. Phase B movement starts with the kids at the back of the classroom, in the same room Phase A just used. This is the smallest join that keeps Game 2’s opening and the same-room rule. The games do not say the kids walk back from the desk.

> Game 2. “The kids are at the back of the classroom.” Merge rule. “Phase B starts immediately in the same room.” Phase A ends with a kid at the desk (Game 1 beat 5), so the two openings cannot both be true unless Phase B begins from the back again.

**REQ-04-018.** In Phase B the kids must get a funny secret note from the back of the classroom to a target mailbox on the other side of the room.

> Game 2. “need to deliver a funny secret note to a target mailbox on the other side of the room.”

**REQ-04-019.** On beat 6, the kids move by stepping on a floor lined with cushions, and they have to guess which cushions are safe to step on.

> Game 2 beat 6. “The floor is lined with cushions.” “The kids have to guess which ones are safe to step on.”

**REQ-04-020.** Three of those cushions have hidden whoopee cushions underneath.

> Game 2 beat 6. “three of them have hidden whoopee cushions underneath.”

**REQ-04-021.** Stepping on a wrong cushion results in a loud, funny noise. That noise is the mistake result beat 6 states.

> Game 2 beat 6. “resulting in loud, funny noises if they pick wrong.”

**REQ-04-022.** On beat 7, the kids pass a giant, oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line, using only their feet.

> Game 2 beat 7. “pass a giant, oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line using only their feet.”

**REQ-04-023.** The sunglasses pass is done without dropping them.

> Game 2 beat 7. “without dropping them.”

**REQ-04-024.** On beat 8, when the teacher turns around to face the board, the kids race to the teacher’s shadow on the wall.

> Game 2 beat 8. “When the teacher turns around to face the board, kids must quickly race to draw … on the teacher’s shadow on the wall.”

**REQ-04-025.** On beat 9, forward movement depends on one kid yelling a silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice. The stated example is “The broccoli has landed!”

> Game 2 beat 9. “To move forward, one kid must yell out a silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice (like ‘The broccoli has landed!’).”

**REQ-04-026.** While the teacher turns around, confused, to investigate that sound, the other kids can crawl forward. The crawl is theirs, not a crawl the yelling sentence gives to the kid who yelled.

> Game 2 beat 9. “While the teacher turns around confused to investigate the sound, the other kids can crawl forward.”

**REQ-04-027.** Phase B’s delivery movement is complete when the secret note reaches the target mailbox.

> Game 2 beat 10. “Once the secret note reaches the target mailbox.”

## 4. Unknowns

- Where beat 1’s “walk across the room” ends. Beats 2–5 still follow, including a mid-way line and the desk, and the source never says how far the squeaky walk goes before beat 2.
- Whether the squeaky toy or bubble wrap stays under the feet after beat 1.
- Whether a kid who was already still when the teacher wakes must also strike a pose. REQ-04-005 names the kids. REQ-04-010 names anyone caught moving.
- Whether every ridiculous pose counts, or only the illustrated one-foot statue. The statue sentence says “like.”
- Whether “they” in the laugh penalty is only the kid who laughed, or every kid in the freeze. Step length is unstated. Whether “back” means toward the rear of the room is unstated.
- Whether a kid may move again immediately after the three steps, and from the new spot.
- How long a freeze lasts, and whether the eyes can open during the 5-second laugh.
- What “caught” means in beat 5, and whether it is the same event as “caught moving” in beat 3.
- Send-back is unstated for beats 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10. That includes the teacher noticing the sticky note, being caught before the desk, a whoopee noise beyond the noise itself, dropping the sunglasses, passing them with anything but feet, racing at the wrong time, yelling the wrong way, or moving other than by crawling during beat 9.
- How many kids there are, and which of them moves on a beat that says “a kid.”
- Who carries the secret note while other kids step, pass, race, or crawl.
- Whether the yelling kid may crawl, stay put, or move in some other way during beat 9. Whether the other kids may walk or run instead of crawl is unstated. “Can crawl” is permission to crawl.
- How many cushions are on the floor, which three hide the whoopee cushions, and whether each of those three hides one whoopee cushion.
- Where the mid-way line is, which wall has the shadow, and which side of the room the mailbox is on beyond “the other side.”
- Phase B states no freeze when the strict teacher looks at the kids. This slice does not copy the Phase A freeze into Phase B.
- The MERGE-CHOICE puts the kids at the back for Phase B. It does not say whether they are placed there or how they leave the desk.
- No point value is stated for a safe step, a freeze, a crawl, a laugh, or a noise. This slice defines none.

## 5. Handoff

- Session win: both finales must happen; this slice stops at reaching the desk without getting caught, and at the note reaching the mailbox.
- Teacher states that open or close a movement window: waking, eyes opening, the 5-second laugh, facing the board, turning to investigate a sound, and standing up as the strict teacher.
- Beat 2 throw: a kid throws a soft plush onto the shield mat. Here, only the resulting 5 seconds in which everyone may move.
- Beat 4 artwork and the stick itself: mustache or goofy-face note on the teacher’s back. Here, the sneak up to the checkpoint and the unnoticed pass.
- Beat 5 prize and confetti: golden trophy, toy, or bucket of treats, and the popper or paper confetti. Here, only arriving at the desk without getting caught.
- Beat 6 layout: who places the cushions and hides the three whoopee cushions. Here, the guess, the step, and the loud funny noise.
- Beat 7 object: the sunglasses as a prop. Here, the feet-only pass down the line without a drop.
- Beat 8 drawing: funny hat or giant ears, drawn with a flashlight. Here, only the race to the shadow when the teacher faces the board.
- Beat 9 voice: how the monster yell is performed beyond the silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice. Here, who yells and who may crawl.
- Beat 10 celebration: gentle silly string on the desk or the shoes, and the victory dance.
