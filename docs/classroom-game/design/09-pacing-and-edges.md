# Pacing and edges

How long each beat should feel, who acts when the text says “a kid” or “the kids,” and what the host does on the edges the requirements leave open. A host can run a session from this file. Felt times are a sense of pace. The only counted time in the session is the 5-second laugh (REQ-MEGA-011, REQ-MEGA-049).

## 1. What this slice designs

- The feel of each beat, and the moment the host calls the next one.
- Turn-taking for “a kid” / “one kid” against “the kids” / “anyone” / “the other kids.”
- The host’s call when two kids start the same action together.
- A recommended group size. The requirements state no kid count (unknown 1). The range below is a recommendation.
- Five open edges: the teacher never stirs, the teacher never turns, the sunglasses are dropped, the sticky note is noticed, a kid laughs during the 5-second window.

This slice does not add a beat, a prop, a score, a second timer, or a send-back. The only send-back in the session remains three steps, for a kid who was caught moving and then laughs (REQ-MEGA-014, REQ-MEGA-040).

The host is the person who calls the beats and names a kid when two kids tie. The teacher is the person in the scene. **RECOMMENDATION.** Use a separate host when the group has an extra person, so the teacher can stay asleep, laughing, or turned away. With no extra person, the teacher speaks the beat call between actions and then returns to the scene.

## 2. The design

### Standing rules the host keeps in every beat

1. Beats run 1 through 10 in that order for the whole group (REQ-MEGA-002). A kid does not open a later beat early.
2. The host calls the next beat when this file’s finish line for the current beat has happened. The host does not add a lap because the room is still laughing.
3. On every edge in this file, the kids stay where they are. The three steps in beat 3 are the only time anyone is sent back.
4. First touch, for a singular action: the kid who gets a hand or a foot on the one prop first does that action. The host says that kid’s name. The other kid stops in place.
5. Same moment: if the host saw no first touch, the kid closer to the back of the room waits, and the kid closer to the desk or the mailbox does the action. The host says the name. Both stay where they are.
6. Together is correct play on a plural action. The host steps in only when two kids would use the one plush, the one sticky note, the one target prize, the one pair of sunglasses, or the one flashlight at the same time.

“One” of each of those objects is the host reading the prop list as it is written. It is not a new prop.

### How long a beat should feel

The session should feel like one sitting: two chapters, ten jokes, no chapter longer than its joke. Phase A should feel like one sneak toward the desk with five jokes in order, not five restarts. Phase B should feel like one trip to the mailbox with five jokes in order. The join between them should feel like walking back to a starting line.

The host does not announce these feels and does not count them down. If the host is wondering whether the beat is finished, the finish line below has already arrived.

| Beat | Finish line the host calls on | How it should feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Squeaky Toy Shoes | The group has walked forward from the back together, and the squeaks have been heard. They are still on the back side of the checkpoint line. | A short noisy parade. The walk is beat 1. The line is beat 4. The desk is beat 5 (REQ-MEGA-041, REQ-MEGA-072, REQ-MEGA-073). |
| 2. Feather Tickle Trigger | The plush is on the mat and the 5 seconds end, or the never-stirs edge below fires, or the teacher started to wake and nobody threw before that window passed. | A held breath, then a scramble or a shrug. |
| 3. Copycat Pose | The teacher has opened their eyes once, anyone who was moving is a statue, and any caught kid who laughed has taken three steps. | A snapshot. The host looks around the group once. |
| 4. Sticky Note Sabotage | One note is on the teacher’s back and the teacher did not notice, and the group has crossed the line. | One dare. |
| 5. Confetti Victory Blast | One kid has reached the desk and claimed the target prize with one blast. | A button. Phase B starts on that claim (REQ-MEGA-020, REQ-MEGA-056). |
| Join | The kids are at the back and the teacher is standing as the strict teacher. | One ordinary walk to the back. No speech, no score, no extra lap. |
| 6. Whoopee Cushion Pathway | The group has crossed to the far side of the cushions. | A step-and-gasp walk. A noise is the joke of a wrong step (REQ-MEGA-060). |
| 7. Reverse Homework Trick | The sunglasses have traveled the line once, each link by a foot pass that did not drop. | One relay. |
| 8. Doodle Attack | One drawing, a hat or giant ears, is on the shadow, or the never-turns edge fires. | A sprint. “Quickly” stays a feel (REQ-MEGA-064). |
| 9. Monster Talk Distraction | One kid has yelled, and the other kids have had a chance to crawl while the teacher looks. | One distraction. |
| 10. Silly String Finale | The secret note is in the mailbox, and the kids have sprayed and danced. | The second button. The session is won when this finale and the confetti blast have both happened (REQ-MEGA-036). |

**RECOMMENDATION — unknown 4.** Play beats 1–5 as one crossing. The host still calls them in order. The group does not return to the back between those beats.

**RECOMMENDATION — unknown 3.** End beat 1 when the squeak has landed and the group has left the back wall, before the checkpoint line. The source does not say how far “across the room” goes before beat 2.

**RECOMMENDATION — the join, unknown 27.** On the claim, the host sends the kids to the back at a normal walk. That walk is the path REQ-MEGA-023 leaves open. It is not a beat and it has no failure. The teacher stands up during that walk and is in the strict-teacher activity when the kids arrive. Beat 6 starts when they are at the back.

**RECOMMENDATION — beat 6, unknowns 7 and 9.** A wrong cushion makes the noise, and the kid stays in the pathway. That kid’s next step is onto a cushion from where they stand. The group keeps going until they are past the cushions. The noise does not move anyone toward the back.

**RECOMMENDATION — beat 9, unknowns 7 and 39.** The yell is enough to finish the beat. The other kids may crawl and may also stay (REQ-MEGA-067). The teacher’s look lasts long enough for a crawl to be worth starting, then the teacher turns back. Zero crawls still finishes the beat.

**RECOMMENDATION — beat 8’s window, unknown 37.** Once the turn has opened the window, it stays open until one drawing is on the shadow or the teacher turns away from the board again. The host then calls beat 9.

**RECOMMENDATION — beat 10.** DESIGN-DECISION 4. The mailbox line is the start of the finale, not a second celebration and not a skip. The host goes straight into the spray and the victory dance. The session cheer is that pair of actions. Beat 10 does not reach backward to skip Phase A. Phase A has already ended on the confetti claim, because the beats stay in order.

### “A kid” and “the kids”

The host does not assign singular beats before play.

**RECOMMENDATION — unknown 2.** The group enters each beat together, in order. The other kids do not each need a personal copy of beats 1–4 before one claim ends Phase A. The host opens beat 5 only after the group’s finish lines for beats 1–4 have happened. Inside beat 5, the first kid who reaches the desk and claims the prize ends Phase A (REQ-MEGA-018, REQ-MEGA-056). Kids who are still behind stay in the session and come to the back for Phase B with everyone else.

| Wording | Who does it | What the others do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “A kid” on the plush (beat 2), the note (beat 4), and the claim (beat 5). “One kid” on the yell (beat 9). | The first kid to start that action. First touch if they start together. | They stay in the beat. They still walk, freeze, crawl, and dance when a plural line says so. They do not repeat the singular action. |
| “The kids” / “kids” on the sneak, the squeaky walk, the wake pose, the cushions, the sunglasses line, the doodle race, the spray, and the dance. | The group, at the same time. | Overlap is the beat. |
| “Anyone caught moving” (beat 3). | Each kid who is moving when the teacher opens their eyes. | A kid who is already still is not caught. DESIGN-DECISION 1. |
| “The kids” on a full wake. | Every kid freezes in a pose, including a kid who was already still. | DESIGN-DECISION 1. The pose is the whole result. The phase continues after the host has looked once. |
| “The other kids” (beat 9). | Everyone except the yeller. | They may crawl during the look. The yeller has already done the yell. |

**RECOMMENDATION — beat 7’s line, unknown 36.** When beat 7 starts, the host stands the kids in one line from the back of the room toward the mailbox and names them in that order. The kid nearest the back starts with the sunglasses on a foot. “Down the line” means toward the mailbox, one kid at a time. If two kids are the same distance from the back, the host names the starter. The kid who waits stays in the line.

**RECOMMENDATION — one unnoticed note, unknown 18.** The first kid who sticks the note without the teacher noticing has passed the line for the group. The other kids cross. They do not each stick another note.

**RECOMMENDATION — the yell.** The first voice is the one yell. A second kid who starts the phrase stops. There is one investigation. The kid who stopped may crawl with the other kids.

### When two kids act at once

The host’s call is the end of the tie. There is no vote and no replay for fairness.

| They both go for | Host call | Where the other kid stays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The plush | First touch throws. One throw puts the plush on the mat and starts the 5 seconds. | In place. The window does not offer a second laugh. |
| The sticky note | First touch makes the approach. One note goes on the back. | In place. No second note. |
| The target prize | First touch claims it and does one blast. Phase A ends. | In place, then to the back with the group. No second blast. |
| The monster yell | First voice is the yell. | Stops the phrase. May crawl. |
| The flashlight | First touch draws the one picture. The race to the shadow can be everyone. | At the shadow, without a second picture. |
| Two cushions at once | Both steps count. Plural beat. | Each kid lives with the cushion they chose. A wrong one makes the noise. |
| The sunglasses, two directions or two passers | The host points to the next named kid in line. | The pair stays with the kid whose turn the line says it is. |
| The spray or the dance | Both keep going. Plural beat. | Side by side. One chosen target, the desk or the shoes (REQ-MEGA-069). |

A kid who starts a singular action during the wrong beat stops and rejoins the group at the group’s current spot. The host says the beat they are actually on. That kid is not sent toward the back.

**RECOMMENDATION — a missed plush, unknown 20.** If the plush misses the mat, the laugh does not start. During the same “starts to wake” window, a kid may throw again from where they stand. When that window passes, the retries stop. A miss does not send anyone back.

### Edge: the teacher never stirs

Beat 2 allows a throw only if the teacher starts to wake (REQ-MEGA-010). The session does not add a wake to force the throw (REQ-MEGA-042). DESIGN-DECISION 1. “Starts to wake” is only this beat’s window.

Host procedure:

1. Call beat 2. The kids watch the teacher. The plush is available. Nobody has to throw.
2. Give the stir one quiet pause: the room settles, and every kid is looking. That pause is the whole wait.
3. If the teacher starts to wake inside the pause, the window is open. A kid may throw. A plush on the mat starts the real 5 seconds. Everyone the window covers may move until those 5 seconds end (REQ-MEGA-049, DESIGN-DECISION 2).
4. If the pause ends and the teacher is still asleep, the window never opened. The plush stays unthrown. The 5 seconds do not happen. The host calls beat 3.
5. The kids stay where they are. Never stirring is not a failure.

A stir after the host has called beat 3 is not a late beat 2. “Starts to wake” does not return. If that later moment is a full wake, every kid freezes in a pose. If it is the eyes opening, anyone still moving becomes a statue (DESIGN-DECISION 1).

**RECOMMENDATION — when the 5 seconds end, unknown 15.** The teacher stops the forced laugh and is sleeping at the desk again. The kids are no longer inside the movement window. They are not frozen by the end of the count itself. They continue from where the 5 seconds left them, and the host calls beat 3. The teacher laughing in place is the teacher’s action for those 5 seconds. The kids are the ones who use the time to move.

### Edge: the teacher never turns

The doodle happens when the teacher turns around to face the board (REQ-MEGA-030). No clap, word, or count replaces that turn (REQ-MEGA-043). DESIGN-DECISION 3. The board is the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the window is a turn away and then a turn back. If the teacher is typing or reading, the window is the turn to face the whiteboard.

Host procedure:

1. Call beat 8 with the kids ready and the flashlight available. Watch for the turn this activity actually needs.
2. Give that turn one quiet pause, the same length as the stir pause. The kids are looking. The teacher’s activity is unchanged.
3. If the turn happens, the kids race and one drawing goes on the shadow. Then call beat 9.
4. If the pause ends without that turn, the kids do not draw. The host does not say “draw now.” The host calls beat 9.
5. The kids stay where they are. Never turning is not a failure. The secret note does not jump to the mailbox because the drawing was skipped. Beat 10 is still last (REQ-MEGA-002, REQ-MEGA-034).

Turning away and never turning back is the same edge as never turning at all: the window did not open. If the turn back happens before the host calls beat 9, the window opened and the group draws.

### Edge: the sunglasses are dropped

The pass is by feet, and the pass the beat names is a pass without a drop (REQ-MEGA-062, REQ-MEGA-063). The beat does not say where a dropped pair returns (unknown 10). A drop is not a written send-back (REQ-MEGA-071, REQ-MEGA-040).

Host procedure:

1. The kids stay in their places in the line. Nobody takes steps toward the back.
2. **RECOMMENDATION — unknown 10.** The sunglasses stay where they fell. They go back onto a foot of the last kid who had them on a foot, at that kid’s spot. That link is tried again. Links that already reached the next kid by a clean foot pass stay done.
3. The beat’s pass is complete when every link has one clean foot pass, from the starter to the mailbox end of the line.
4. A hand on the sunglasses is an unfinished link. The host says “feet.” The pair returns to the last foot, at that spot, and that link is tried again. Nobody is sent back.
5. Another drop on the same link uses the same return. The line does not restart from the back.

### Edge: the sticky note is noticed

Passing the checkpoint line requires a kid to stick the note on the teacher’s back and requires that the teacher does not notice (REQ-MEGA-052, REQ-MEGA-053). The beat states no send-back if the teacher notices (unknown 8, REQ-MEGA-071).

Host procedure:

1. One kid approaches with the one note.
2. If the teacher does not notice, the note stays. The group crosses. The host calls beat 5 when they are past the line.
3. If the teacher notices, that try does not pass the line. The note comes off the back. The kids stay where they are. Any kid may try again from the group’s current spot, still during beat 4.
4. The host does not skip the line. The requirement to pass is an unnoticed note, so beat 4 stays open until one try is unnoticed. Each try should feel like one dare. A noticed try ends that dare at once.
5. Noticing is a feel during sleep. It is not “starts to wake,” not “wakes up,” and not “opens their eyes” (DESIGN-DECISION 1). Beat 3 has already used the eyes. If the teacher actually opens their eyes or wakes fully during beat 4, the host uses that moment’s freeze, then returns the teacher to sleep and continues the note tries. The freeze is not three steps unless a kid who was caught moving then laughs.

**RECOMMENDATION — unknown 18, what the teacher does.** The sleeping teacher shifts enough that the host and the kids can tell the note was felt, then settles back to sleep. The shift is the whole reaction.

### Edge: a kid laughs during the 5-second window

The 5 seconds are the teacher’s laugh after the plush lands. DESIGN-DECISION 2. During those 5 seconds the kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them.

Host procedure:

1. Start the five-count when the plush is on the mat. A kid’s laugh does not pause the count, does not add a second count, and does not freeze the group.
2. A kid who laughs inside the five is not taking the three steps. The three steps belong to beat 3, and only to a kid who was caught moving and then laughs (REQ-MEGA-014). During the five, movement is allowed, so those kids are not “caught.”
3. The teacher’s own laugh is not a kid’s laugh. Nobody steps back because the teacher is laughing.
4. If the teacher wakes fully or opens their eyes inside the five, the freeze waits until the five end (DESIGN-DECISION 2). Then it applies. A full wake freezes every kid in a pose. Open eyes make a statue of anyone who is still moving when the five end (DESIGN-DECISION 1).
5. A laugh that continues after the five, from a kid who was caught moving at that eyes-open moment, is the three steps. A laugh that finished inside the five does not become those steps later.
6. **RECOMMENDATION — unknown 11.** Any kid-laugh that is not “caught moving, then laughed” leaves the kid where they are. That includes a laugh in the 5-second window, a laugh from a kid who was already still, and a laugh during the walk.

**RECOMMENDATION — after the three steps, unknown 12.** “Back” faces the back of the room, where Phase A started. A step is one ordinary step that kid already uses. The host does not measure it. After the third step the kid stops there. Beat 3 does not restart. Beats 1 and 2 do not restart. When the host calls beat 4, that kid continues with the group from that spot.

**RECOMMENDATION — how long the pose or the statue lasts, unknown 14.** The host looks around the group once, long enough to see a laugh from a caught kid. Then the host moves the session on. A kid who does not freeze is told the pose or the statue once and takes it where they stand. A kid who was already still on a full wake still takes a pose. A kid who was already still when the eyes open is not caught and does not take the statue.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

- **DESIGN-DECISION 1.** Three wake moments. “Starts to wake” is only the beat 2 throw window. “Wakes up” freezes every kid in a pose. “Opens their eyes” is the statue for anyone still moving. Used for the never-stirs edge, for a stir that arrives after beat 2, for a noticed note that must not become a fourth wake, and for who freezes.
- **DESIGN-DECISION 2.** During the 5-second laugh, kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them. Used for the kid-laugh edge, and for a wake or an eyes-open that happens inside the five: the freeze waits until the count ends.
- **DESIGN-DECISION 3.** The board is the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the doodle window is a turn away and then a turn back. If the teacher is typing or reading, the window is the turn to face the whiteboard. Used for the never-turns edge. No replacement signal.
- **DESIGN-DECISION 4.** Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales. “Win the game” in beat 10 does not skip Phase A. Used for the finale’s feel: mailbox, then spray and dance, as one ending.

## 5. Requirement fixes

None in this slice. The open edges are unknowns the requirements already mark. They are filled here only as labeled recommendations. The three-step rule is left as the only send-back.

## 6. Open questions this slice refused to guess

- A second counted time. No beat receives a clock. The 5-second laugh stays the only one.
- A measured step, a measured room, or a spot for the mat, the line, the mailbox, or the shadow beyond “back,” “front,” and “toward the mailbox.”
- An age, a grade, or a rule that forbids a group outside 4 to 8. The range is a recommendation.
- Whether a statue, a sticky-note picture, or a monster phrase other than the named examples counts.
- Whether one shadow drawing may show both a hat and giant ears. The host ends beat 8 on one drawing that is a hat or giant ears.
- The words on the secret note, who carries it, and which action moves it. Skipping the doodle does not deliver it. This slice does not invent the carry.
- The steps of the victory dance.
- Whether the party popper also travels over the teacher’s head, and whether one claim uses both blast objects. The claim uses one blast.
- What “safe,” “soft,” and “gentle” require beyond those words.
- What happens when the spray is not gentle, or hits something other than the desk or the shoes. The host points them back at the chosen target. No send-back is added, and no further result is invented.
- Whether a later failure undoes a claimed prize or a finished finale.
- Replay of a beat, a phase, or a real choice.
- Which three cushions hide a whoopee cushion, and how many cushions line the floor.
- What casts the teacher’s shadow.
