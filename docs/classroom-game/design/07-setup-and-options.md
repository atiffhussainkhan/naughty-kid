# Setup and options

## 1. What this slice designs

This is the sheet a host uses before one session, in one room.

It designs the five closed picks the host makes before Phase A: prize type, shoe noisemaker, victory effect, what the strict teacher is doing, and the silly-string target.

It marks three source samples as examples the host may replace: the statue, the sticky-note picture, and the monster phrase.

It designs the only written rebuild: three steps back.

It recommends how the group gets from the teacher’s desk to the back of the room when Phase B opens. That path is a recommendation.

A host can run the session from section 2. Sections 3–6 separate design decisions, recommendations, and the gaps left open.

## 2. The design

### The host

RECOMMENDATION. The source names no picker. One person makes the five picks and places what those picks need. This sheet calls that person the host. The teacher stays the one teacher in the room: asleep at the desk in Phase A, then standing as the strict teacher. The host may be that teacher.

### Five picks, before Phase A

The host makes each pick before Phase A starts. Phase A still starts with the teacher sleeping at the teacher’s desk at the front, guarding the prize, and the kids at the back of the room.

Each pick is one option from its own list. The source names no favorite, so the host chooses. All five are chosen before the kids start.

RECOMMENDATION. Each pick lasts through both finales. The same five stay in force for Phase A and Phase B.

| Pick | The host chooses one | Where that choice is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prize type | A golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats | The one target prize on the teacher’s desk. A kid claims it at that desk. REQ-MEGA-005, REQ-MEGA-018 |
| Shoe noisemaker | A squeaky dog toy, or bubble wrap | Under the feet on the beat 1 walk across the room. Sneaking silently is impossible on that walk. REQ-MEGA-048, REQ-MEGA-008, REQ-MEGA-009 |
| Victory effect | Pop a safe party popper, or throw a handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head | The blast used as the kid claims the prize. That claim ends Phase A. REQ-MEGA-054, REQ-MEGA-055, REQ-MEGA-019, REQ-MEGA-056 |
| Strict-teacher activity | Standing facing a whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book | What the teacher does, while pretending to be very strict, from the moment they stand up. REQ-MEGA-057, REQ-MEGA-022, REQ-MEGA-021 |

How the host lays each pick:

1. **Prize.** The host sets the chosen prize on the teacher’s desk. That object is the target prize the sleeping teacher guards. A golden trophy is one prize. A toy is one prize. A bucket of treats is one prize.

2. **Shoe noisemaker.** The host puts the chosen kind under the feet for the beat 1 walk. The beat keeps the name The Squeaky Toy Shoes with either kind. RECOMMENDATION. Every kid on that walk uses the one kind the host picked.

3. **Victory effect.** The host sets out the one blast the claim will use. Popping the safe party popper is the blast. Throwing a handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head is the blast. Either action is the confetti victory blast. The paper-confetti throw is the action that goes over the teacher’s head. Phase A ends when a kid claims the prize with the blast the host picked.

4. **Strict-teacher activity.** The host tells the teacher which activity they will perform. In every activity the teacher pretends to be very strict. The teacher begins that activity when they stand up at the opening of Phase B. RECOMMENDATION. The teacher keeps that activity until the doodle window.

   DESIGN-DECISION. The board is the whiteboard. The activity pick tells the teacher how the doodle window opens.
   - Standing facing the whiteboard: the window opens when the teacher turns away and then turns back.
   - Typing on a computer, or reading a big book: the window opens when the teacher turns to face the whiteboard.

5. **Silly-string target.** The host names the desk or the shoes before the session. In the finale the kids gently spray that target and do a victory dance. The dance goes with the spray. The desk in this pick is the teacher’s desk carried from Phase A (REQ-MEGA-025). The shoes in this pick are the teacher’s shoes. The noisemaker under the kids’ feet is the beat 1 pick. The teacher’s shoes are the finale target.

RECOMMENDATION. The host puts the chosen option into play for each pick. One prize is guarded. One noisemaker kind is under the feet. One blast is used on the claim. One activity is performed. One surface receives the spray.

### The only rebuild

The only written rebuild is three steps back (REQ-MEGA-014, REQ-MEGA-040, REQ-MEGA-075).

When the teacher opens their eyes, anyone caught moving freezes and imitates the statue, the sample or the host’s replacement. If that kid laughs, that kid takes three steps back.

The host runs it in that order:

1. The teacher has opened their eyes.
2. A kid who was caught moving is holding the statue.
3. That kid laughs.
4. The host counts three steps back for that kid.

RECOMMENDATION. Back is toward the back of the room, the end where Phase A started. Each step is one step of that kid’s own walk. The source gives no length.

RECOMMENDATION. After the third step, the kid stays where that step landed. The session continues from there. The five picks stay as the host made them. Beat 3, Phase A, and the session continue.

DESIGN-DECISION. During the teacher’s 5-second laugh, the kids may move. That laugh is the one a soft plush on the shield mat forces. The three steps belong to a different laugh: the kid who was caught moving, during the eyes-open statue. Outside those 5 seconds, a wake freezes the kids. The host counts three steps for the statue laugh above.

The steps belong to the kid the rule names: a kid who was caught moving, and who laughs.

The three steps stay in that beat 3 laugh. A wrong cushion in beat 6 makes the loud noise that beat writes (REQ-MEGA-060). The host leaves the group on that noise.

On every other beat, the host uses a send-back only where that beat already writes one. This session writes one. Where a beat writes no failure, the host adds none (REQ-MEGA-071, REQ-MEGA-039).

### Return to the back for Phase B

Phase A ends at the teacher’s desk, on the claim with the victory blast. Phase B starts immediately, in the same room (REQ-MEGA-020). The teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher (REQ-MEGA-021). Phase B opens with the kids at the back of the room, including this moment after the desk (REQ-MEGA-023). Props and the room stay (REQ-MEGA-024).

RECOMMENDATION. The host returns the group like this:

1. The victory blast has just ended.
2. The host tells the kids to walk to the back of the room, the place Phase A started.
3. The kids walk there. The teacher stands up and begins the strict activity already picked.
4. The claimed prize, the desk, and the rest of the room stay where they are. The host already placed the Phase B objects before the session, so the walk has nothing to build.
5. When the kids are at the back, the group plays beats 6 through 10 in order.

This is how one group can open Phase B at the back after ending Phase A at the desk. It is a recommendation. The source writes the opening position and leaves the path unwritten.

DESIGN-DECISION. Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after the confetti victory blast and the silly string finale have both happened. The walk to the back changes where the kids stand. The five picks change what is set out. Beat 10’s “win the game” leaves Phase A in place, already played, and leaves the silly-string finale in place as the end of Phase B.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

1. **Three wake moments.** Used for the statue example. The replaceable statue is the eyes-open copycat. “Starts to wake” stays the plush window. “Wakes up” stays the pose freeze. The host is given no menu of those moments.

2. **Kids may move during the 5-second laugh.** Used so the host keeps that window separate from the three steps. The rebuild is the kid’s laugh inside the eyes-open statue. Outside the 5 seconds, a wake freezes the kids.

3. **The board is the whiteboard.** Used on the strict-teacher activity. Facing the whiteboard: the doodle window is turn away, then turn back. Typing or reading: the doodle window is the turn to face the whiteboard.

4. **Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales.** Used on the return to the back. The return sits between the desk claim and beat 6. Beat 10’s “win the game” leaves Phase A already played and leaves the finale as the end of Phase B.

## 5. Requirement fixes

In the core’s examples table, section 5 joins “a drawing of a mustache, or a drawing of a goofy face” with “or.” That is the same join the core uses for a real choice. REQ-MEGA-017 calls both drawings source examples introduced with “like.” A later pass should keep both drawings as examples, so a host does not treat them as a closed pick of two.

Nothing else in this slice’s scope is a duplicate or a broken requirement. The popper and the confetti throw already have their own statements (REQ-MEGA-054, REQ-MEGA-055), the blast name sits on REQ-MEGA-019, and “over the teacher’s head” stays on the paper-confetti throw.

- Whether a kid who was not caught moving, and who laughs, takes three steps back.

- The words on the secret note, and the movements of the victory dance.
