# Slice 05 — Movement and failure

Host card for how kids move, when they stop, and what a mistake does. One room, one teacher, beats in the written order. The only written send-back is three steps back after a laugh from a kid who was caught moving (REQ-MEGA-014, REQ-MEGA-040). Every other mistake stays where it happened. Host actions under a silent beat are marked RECOMMENDATION. They are not new failure rules (REQ-MEGA-039, REQ-MEGA-071).

## 1. What this slice designs

- Phase A sneaking: the walk from the back, the checkpoint, and the reach to the desk.
- The wake pose, the copycat statue, and the 5-second move window.
- The three-step send-back.
- The wrong whoopee step, the sunglasses foot-pass, and the crawl during the monster yell.
- The race to the shadow, only as a movement window.
- What the host does when a mistake has no written result.

Out of this slice: the words on the note, the doodle itself, the prize claim’s popper or confetti, the silly-string spray, and the victory dance. This slice ends those travels at the desk and at the mailbox.

## 2. The design

### Two phases, two movement laws

Phase A is a sneak from the back of the room to the teacher’s desk. The teacher is sleeping there, guarding the target prize (REQ-MEGA-004, REQ-MEGA-046, REQ-MEGA-006, REQ-MEGA-047).

Phase B opens with the kids at the back of the same room (REQ-MEGA-023). They carry a secret note to the mailbox on the other side (REQ-MEGA-026, REQ-MEGA-058). Beats stay in order: 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 (REQ-MEGA-002).

Phase A’s pose, statue, and three steps are Game 1 rules. The host does not use them as Phase B penalties. Phase B’s written mistake result is the whoopee noise (REQ-MEGA-060).

When Phase A ends, the kid who claimed the prize is at the desk, and Phase B still opens at the back (REQ-MEGA-023). RECOMMENDATION: the host asks the kids to walk to the back while the teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher. That walk has no freeze and no send-back. Beat 6 starts when they are at the back.

DESIGN-DECISION: beat 10 wins Phase B. The note in the mailbox starts the silly-string finale. The session is won only after the confetti victory blast and that finale have both happened. Mailing the note does not skip Phase A.

### Host calls

The host speaks these calls. Kid noises do not force them. The monster yell is the one written exception: it turns the teacher to investigate (REQ-MEGA-033). The plush on the mat is the other: it starts the laugh (REQ-MEGA-011).

| Call | When the host may say it | What the kids do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Starting to wake.” | Phase A, any time the teacher is sleeping. This call is only the plush window. | A kid may throw the soft plush onto the shield mat (REQ-MEGA-010). The host does not add a wake to force the throw (REQ-MEGA-042). |
| “Five.” | The plush is on the mat. The host counts five seconds aloud. | Kids may move for those five seconds (REQ-MEGA-049). DESIGN-DECISION: this window is the exception to the wake freeze. |
| “Wakes up.” | Phase A, outside that five-second count. | Every kid freezes in a ridiculous pose (REQ-MEGA-007). DESIGN-DECISION. |
| “Eyes open.” | Phase A, outside that five-second count. | Anyone still moving becomes a statue (REQ-MEGA-012, REQ-MEGA-050). DESIGN-DECISION. |
| “Asleep.” | The host ends a wake pose. | Kids may sneak again from the spots they held. |
| “Eyes shut.” | The host ends a statue. | Caught kids who did not laugh may sneak again from where they froze. |
| “I noticed.” | Beat 4, the sleeping teacher reacts to the sticky note. | That note does not pass the line (REQ-MEGA-053). |
| “Turn.” | Beat 8, the teacher faces the board. | Kids may race to the shadow (REQ-MEGA-064). DESIGN-DECISION on when the turn is real. |
| “Listen.” | Beat 9, just after the monster yell. | The teacher turns to investigate. The other kids may crawl (REQ-MEGA-067). |
| “Back.” | The host ends the investigation. | Crawling stops. Kids keep the ground a crawl gained. |

“Asleep,” “Eyes shut,” “I noticed,” “Turn,” “Listen,” and “Back” are host cues. RECOMMENDATION: none of them is a timer. The five-second count is the only clock in the session (REQ-MEGA-011).

DESIGN-DECISION: “Starting to wake,” “Wakes up,” and “Eyes open” are three calls. The host does not stack them into one moment.

RECOMMENDATION: the host finishes the five-second count before saying “Wakes up” or “Eyes open.” During the count, kids keep moving even if the host was about to call a wake.

### Sneaking

Phase A is one crossing in beat order. RECOMMENDATION: the host runs it as one trip, not five separate races back to the wall.

1. **Beat 1 walk.** The kids walk from the back. Each walker has a squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap under a foot (REQ-MEGA-008, REQ-MEGA-048). A silent sneak is impossible (REQ-MEGA-009). The sound is the walk. The host does not grade how funny it is. Finishing the walk is not the prize claim (REQ-MEGA-041).
2. **Beat 2 window.** On “Starting to wake,” a kid may throw the plush. If it lands on the mat, the host counts five and the kids may move (REQ-MEGA-049). They may also stand still. The count still finishes.
3. **Beat 3 check.** On “Eyes open,” movement becomes the statue. See below.
4. **Beat 4 line.** The room has a mid-way checkpoint line (REQ-MEGA-016). Passing it takes a sneak to the teacher, a sticky note on the teacher’s back, and no notice (REQ-MEGA-051, REQ-MEGA-052, REQ-MEGA-053). The line stays on beat 4 (REQ-MEGA-072).
5. **Beat 5 desk.** A kid who reaches the desk without getting caught claims the target prize (REQ-MEGA-018, REQ-MEGA-073). The claim itself is the other slice.

RECOMMENDATION for the line and the walk: the host marks one line on the floor between the back wall and the desk. “Mid-way” is the name of that line. The beat 1 walk ends on the near side of it, the side toward the back wall. Kids stay on that side until an unnoticed note is on the teacher’s back.

RECOMMENDATION for who sneaks the note and who claims: any one kid may throw the plush, stick the note, or reach the desk. The other kids still walk on beat 1 and still freeze on a wake. Phase A ends when one kid claims with the victory blast (REQ-MEGA-056). The others do not have to reach the desk first.

RECOMMENDATION for the under-foot item: the host gives each walker one of the two items for the beat 1 walk. One kind for the whole group is enough. When the host says the walk has reached the line, the kids take the items out from under their feet. Later beats do not keep the toy or the wrap underfoot.

RECOMMENDATION for a note that sticks: one unnoticed note opens the line for every kid still on the near side. They may cross. The host says nothing if the teacher does not notice.

The sticky-note pictures stay examples. RECOMMENDATION: this session the host accepts a mustache drawing or a face drawing. The host does not judge the drawing’s tone.

### Freezing

DESIGN-DECISION: “Wakes up” freezes every kid in a ridiculous pose. That includes a kid who was already standing still. The pose is that kid’s own still shape. The host does not score it.

The host says “Wakes up,” then waits until every kid is still. Play stays paused until then. The host says “Asleep,” and sneaking continues from those spots.

DESIGN-DECISION: during the five-second laugh, kids may move. Outside that count, a wake freezes them.

A wake is a stop. It is not “caught,” and it is not the three steps.

### The copycat statue

DESIGN-DECISION: “Eyes open” is the copycat statue for anyone still moving.

The host says “Eyes open” and looks once. Each kid whose body is still changing position at that look is caught. Each kid who is already still is not caught. The host leaves an already-still kid in the shape they hold.

A caught kid instantly imitates a statue (REQ-MEGA-012, REQ-MEGA-050). The source example is balancing on one foot with a finger on the nose (REQ-MEGA-013). RECOMMENDATION: the host shows that statue once, before Phase A starts, and asks a caught kid to take it. If the kid is still in some other shape, the host accepts the stillness for this session. No written result covers a different shape, so the host does not add one.

Caught kids hold the statue until “Eyes shut,” unless they laugh. An already-still kid does not wait on that word. They were free to be still, and they stay free to sneak when the host’s look is over. RECOMMENDATION: the host’s look is the one glance on the word “Eyes open.” A kid who moves after that glance is caught too, until “Eyes shut.”

RECOMMENDATION for beat 5 “caught”: the host uses this same catch. A kid gets caught when “Eyes open” finds them moving. A wake pose is not that catch. A kid who reaches the desk while not caught claims the prize. A past statue that already ended does not cancel a later arrival. A kid who is caught short of the desk does not claim. They are in the statue at that spot.

### The three-step send-back

This is the only send-back the games write (REQ-MEGA-040). It is the only rebuild the host uses.

Trigger: a kid who was caught moving laughs (REQ-MEGA-014). Each caught kid who laughs takes their own three steps. A caught kid who does not laugh stays in the statue and does not step back.

RECOMMENDATION for the laugh: an audible laugh from that kid, after they are caught and before “Eyes shut.” A silent smile does not count. A laugh during the five-second count does not count, because that kid is not caught then.

RECOMMENDATION for the steps:

- The three steps start as soon as that kid laughs. The host allows those steps while the eyes are still open. The host does not statue that kid a second time for those three steps.
- One step is one footfall of that kid.
- “Back” faces the back wall, the wall where Phase A started.
- Then the kid stops. They are still in the session.
- Other kids stay where they are. A note already unnoticed stays on the teacher’s back. A prize already claimed stays claimed. A beat the group has already left stays finished.
- If the kid is already against the back wall, the remaining steps are steps in place.
- If a prop blocks the straight line, the kid steps to the side of it and still counts three footfalls toward the back wall.
- After the three steps, that kid waits for “Eyes shut,” then sneaks again from the new spot. If the steps carried them back across the checkpoint, they are on the near side again. The line stays open if an unnoticed note is already on the teacher’s back. They walk up and cross. They do not stick a second note.

A kid who was not caught, and who laughs, has no written result. RECOMMENDATION: the host does nothing. That kid takes no steps.

### The wrong whoopee step

Beat 6. The floor is lined with cushions. Three of them have a hidden whoopee cushion underneath (REQ-MEGA-027, REQ-MEGA-059). The kids guess which cushions are safe to step on (REQ-MEGA-028). A wrong pick makes a loud noise (REQ-MEGA-060). The host does not grade the noise.

The written result is that noise. Send-back: none.

RECOMMENDATION for the layout: the host lays the cushions in a path from the back toward the mailbox, using more cushions than three, and hides one whoopee under each of three cushions. The host does not point out which three. A whoopee that has already sounded stays under its cushion.

RECOMMENDATION for the carrier: at the Phase B opening the host hands the secret note to one kid. The note moves when that kid moves. Hands may hold the note. The feet rule belongs to the sunglasses, not to the note.

RECOMMENDATION after the noise: the kid stays on the cushion that sounded. They may guess a different cushion on their next step. The pathway continues. Beat 6 ends when the note carrier steps off the far end of the cushions, on the mailbox side. Other kids may still be on the path. The note is not put in the mailbox on this beat.

A quiet step has no written result. RECOMMENDATION: the kid stays on that cushion. The host does not announce a score.

### The sunglasses foot-pass

Beat 7. The kids pass a giant, oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line, using only their feet, without dropping them (REQ-MEGA-061, REQ-MEGA-062, REQ-MEGA-063). The pass is what they do instead of work (REQ-MEGA-029). The host does not add a worksheet.

RECOMMENDATION for the line: the host stands the kids in one line from the back of the room toward the mailbox. The pair starts on the floor at the feet of the kid at the back end. “Down the line” runs toward the mailbox. A pass is a foot pushing, dragging, or toeing the pair onto the next kid’s feet. Hands stay off the pair. The note, if this kid is carrying it, stays in a hand.

Beat 7 ends when the kid at the mailbox end has the pair resting on their feet.

RECOMMENDATION if only one kid is in the line: that kid passes the pair from one foot to the other and back, without a drop. The host then ends the beat. This does not pick a class size.

A drop has no written result. The pass is written “without dropping.” RECOMMENDATION: the host puts the pair on the floor at the feet of the last kid who held it, and the pass continues from that kid. Every kid stays in their place in line.

A pass that uses a hand, a knee, or anything other than a foot has no written result. RECOMMENDATION: that pass does not move the pair down the line. The host returns the pair to the last feet-only rest and they try again from that kid. The line of bodies stays put.

### Crawling during the monster yell

Beat 9. To move forward, one kid yells a phrase in a deep monster voice (REQ-MEGA-031). The source example is “The broccoli has landed!” (REQ-MEGA-032). On that yell the teacher turns, confused, to investigate (REQ-MEGA-033). While the teacher is turned, the other kids can crawl forward (REQ-MEGA-067).

RECOMMENDATION: the host uses the example phrase for this session. The yelling kid is any one kid. The host says “Listen,” and turns to look toward the yell. The other kids may crawl toward the mailbox while the teacher is turned. The host says “Back,” and turns around again. There is no clock on the look.

RECOMMENDATION: a crawl is on hands and knees, toward the mailbox. Ground gained by a crawl is kept when the host says “Back.” Beat 9 ends on “Back.” The crawl is allowed. The beat still ends if nobody crawled. The yell is what opened the window.

The sentence gives the crawl to the other kids. RECOMMENDATION: the host does not invite the yelling kid to crawl. If that kid crawls anyway, the host leaves them wherever the crawl ends. There is no written result for that crawl.

Walking, running, or traveling upright during “Listen” has no written result. RECOMMENDATION: the host tells that kid to stop at the spot they are on. They may drop and crawl from there until “Back.” The host does not walk them back to where they started, and does not use the Phase A pose or the three steps.

If nobody yells, the window does not open. RECOMMENDATION: the host waits. Kids stay where they are.

The note can travel during the crawl if a crawling kid is holding it. It still goes into the mailbox on beat 10, which stays last (REQ-MEGA-002). RECOMMENDATION: if someone puts the note in the mailbox early, the host takes it out, returns it to the kid who was carrying it, and the finale does not start.

### The race, as a window only

Beat 8. The kids race to the teacher’s shadow on the wall when the teacher turns to face the board (REQ-MEGA-030, REQ-MEGA-064). The drawing is the other slice.

DESIGN-DECISION: the board is the whiteboard.

- If the strict teacher is already facing the whiteboard, the window opens when they turn away and then turn back.
- If the strict teacher is typing or reading, the window opens when they turn to face the whiteboard.

The host says “Turn” only on that real turn. Kids race from wherever they are standing. The host points at one shadow they can reach. RECOMMENDATION: a move toward the shadow before “Turn” does not count as the race. The kids stay where that early move stopped. They race from there when “Turn” is real. The host may set up another real turn later in the beat. Bodies are not walked backward.

A kid who does not race has no written result. RECOMMENDATION: the host leaves them where they are. Kids who do race may go on to the drawing.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

1. Three wake moments. “Starting to wake” is only the plush window. “Wakes up” freezes every kid in a pose, including a kid who was already still. “Eyes open” is the copycat statue for anyone still moving. Used in the host calls, the freeze, the statue, and the beat 5 catch.
2. During the 5-second laugh, kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them. Used so the count is never cut short by a freeze, and so a laugh during the count is not the three-step trigger.
3. The board is the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the race window opens when they turn away and then turn back. If they are typing or reading, the window opens when they turn to face the whiteboard. Used only for when the race may start.
4. Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales. “Win the game” on the mailbox does not skip Phase A. Used so an early mailbox drop does not end the session, and so the mailbox is the Phase B win at beat 10.

## 4. Recommendations for UNKNOWN facts

These keep one group moving. They do not change the requirements.

- The host places the checkpoint line and treats the beat 1 walk as ending on its near side. “Mid-way” is not measured (unknowns 3, 4, 19).
- Any one kid may throw, stick the note, claim, or yell. The group does not wait for every kid to reach the desk (unknown 2).
- The under-foot item is for the beat 1 walk. Then it comes out from under the feet (unknown 26).
- One unnoticed note opens the line for the whole group. “I noticed” voids that note only. Kids already across stay across (unknown 18).
- “Caught” at the desk means “Eyes open” found that kid moving. A finished statue does not erase a later arrival (unknown 17).
- Freeze and statue last until the host’s release word. No second clock (unknown 14).
- The host does not overlap “Wakes up” or “Eyes open” with the five-second count (unknown 15).
- The host cues every kid on the count. The teacher stays at the desk and laughs. This does not define the word “everyone” (unknown 15).
- An audible laugh is the laugh. Three steps use that kid’s footfalls toward the Phase A back wall. Then they sneak again from there. A laugh from a kid who was not caught does nothing (unknowns 11, 12).
- Kid noises do not force “Starting to wake,” “Wakes up,” or “Eyes open.” The host chooses those calls (unknown 16).
- This session teaches the one-foot statue and the broccoli phrase, and accepts a different still shape because no penalty is written for it (unknowns 21, and the phrase half of unknown 21).
- After the claim, the host walks everyone to the back. Pose, statue, and three steps stop when the teacher stands up (unknowns 27, 50).
- Cushion path: more than three cushions, one whoopee under each of three, left in place after they sound. Beat 6 ends when the note carrier steps off the far end. A wrong step stays a noise (unknowns 7, 9, 35).
- One kid carries the note in a hand. It follows that kid through beats 6–9 and enters the mailbox on beat 10 (unknown 40).
- Sunglasses: one line, back end toward the mailbox end, feet only. A drop or a bad touch returns the pair to the last legal feet. Bodies stay put. One kid passes foot to foot (unknowns 10, 36).
- Crawl is hands and knees toward the mailbox, allowed and not required. Upright travel stops in place. The yeller’s extra crawl is left where it ends. “Back” ends the beat (unknowns 7, 39).
- An early race does not count and does not move anyone backward. The host points at one reachable shadow (unknowns 37, 38).
- A later mistake does not undo a claimed prize, an unnoticed note, or a beat already left (unknown 44).
- If a carried prop sits on a cushion the kids must step on, or in the foot-pass line, the host slides that prop aside before the beat. That slide is setup (unknown 34).

## 5. Requirement fixes

None. REQ-MEGA-040 already keeps the three steps as the only written send-back, and REQ-MEGA-060 already gives the wrong step its own result, the noise. The design uses both as written.

## 6. Open questions this design refused to guess

The recommendations above tell a host what to do tonight. They do not close these gaps in the requirements:

- How many kids are in the session.
- A measured step, room, or “mid-way.”
- Whether the word “everyone” includes the teacher.
- Whether any statue, sticky-note picture, or monster phrase besides the named examples meets the requirement.
- Whether “Eyes open” can legally overlap the five-second laugh.
- What “caught” means in the source, beyond the host’s working rule that it is the eyes-open catch.
- Which kid action, if any, makes the teacher start to wake, wake up, or open their eyes.
- How long a freeze lasts, as a fact of the text rather than a host word.
- Where a dropped pair of sunglasses returns, as a fact of the text.
- Whether a cushion with no whoopee is defined as safe, and how many cushions exist.
- Whether the yelling kid is allowed to crawl, as a fact of the text.
- What the secret note says, what casts the shadow, and what the teacher does after a noise or a finale.
- Replay of a beat, a phase, or the session, and whether the teacher has a win.
- What “safe,” “soft,” and “gentle” require beyond those words.
