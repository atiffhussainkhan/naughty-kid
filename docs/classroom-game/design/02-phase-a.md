# Phase A play — beats 1 through 5

Host script for one room and one teacher. Phase A is Game 1. The beats run in order: The Squeaky Toy Shoes, The Feather Tickle Trigger, The Copycat Pose, The Sticky Note Sabotage, The Confetti Victory Blast. Phase A ends when a kid claims the target prize with the confetti victory blast. The session continues. It is won only after that blast and the later silly-string finale have both happened.

A line tagged **Requirement** is already in the core document. A line tagged **DESIGN-DECISION** is one of the four decisions in the design brief. A line tagged **Recommendation** is the smallest rule that lets one group play an UNKNOWN. It is not a requirement.

## 1. What this slice designs

How a teacher hosts beats 1 through 5 from a cold start through the Phase A claim.

For each beat: the start condition, what the kids do, what the teacher does, how the beat ends, and the mistake. Beat 3 is the only Phase A beat with a written mistake result. The other four mistake results are recommendations.

This slice stops when Phase A ends. It does not host Phase B, and it does not move the kids back from the desk.

## 2. The design

### Room, before beat 1

**Requirement.** The teacher is sleeping at the teacher’s desk at the front of the room, guarding the target prize. The kids are at the back of the room. They are here to sneak from the back and grab that prize.

**Recommendation.** The teacher is the host. Before beat 1 the teacher locks three choices and leaves the other options unset:

- One target prize on the desk: a golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats.
- One under-foot object for the walk: a squeaky dog toy, or bubble wrap.
- One victory blast, set where the claiming kid can reach it at the desk: a safe party popper, or a handful of paper confetti.

The teacher also places a soft plush toy with the kids, points to one mat on the floor and names it the designated shield mat, and marks one line on the floor between the back of the room and the desk and names it the mid-way checkpoint line. The sticky note already shows one source example: a mustache, or a goofy face. The teacher stays at the desk for all five beats. Standing up belongs to the start of Phase B.

The mat sits where a kid, still on the back side of the line, can throw the plush onto it. The line is a mark the group can see. Nothing is measured.

### Shared wake rules

Three teacher moments stay separate. **DESIGN-DECISION.** “Starts to wake” is only the beat 2 throw window. “Wakes up” freezes every kid in a ridiculous pose. “Opens their eyes” is the beat 3 statue for anyone still moving.

**DESIGN-DECISION.** During the 5-second laugh, the kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them.

**Requirement.** “Immediately” and “instantly” are not timed. The only duration in Phase A is the 5-second laugh. The pose and the statue are source tone. The teacher does not grade them. The only send-back Phase A writes is three steps back, and only for a kid who was caught moving and then laughs.

**Recommendation.** The squeaks, the pop of bubble wrap, and any other noise do not cause a wake, a start-to-wake, or the eyes to open. The teacher chooses those moments. A pose or a statue lasts until the teacher is sleeping again. Then the kids may move, still inside the current beat. A kid who is late to freeze is told to freeze and stays in place. A kid who was already still when the eyes open is not caught and does not strike the statue. On a wake, every kid freezes, including a kid who was already still.

### Beat 1 — The Squeaky Toy Shoes

**Start.** The room above is set. The teacher is sleeping and guarding the prize. The kids are at the back, each with the chosen under-foot object.

**Kids.** They walk from the back toward the front with that object under their feet. Silent sneaking is impossible on this walk. The sound happens because of the object. The teacher does not grade the sound. The target prize stays unclaimed when the walk finishes.

**Teacher.** Sleeps at the desk. This beat does not use “starts to wake” and does not use “opens their eyes.” If the teacher wakes up, every kid freezes in a pose until the teacher is sleeping again. The walk then continues.

**End.** **Recommendation.** The teacher ends the walk when every kid who is walking has walked with the object under their feet and the group is still on the back side of the checkpoint line. Beat 4 still owns that line. Beat 5 still owns the desk. The under-foot object comes out from under the feet when the walk ends. Beat 2 starts.

**Mistake.** **Requirement.** Beat 1 states none. **Recommendation.** A kid without the object under their feet has not done this walk. They put it under their feet and walk. The kids stay where the freeze or the walk left them. Nobody takes steps back.

### Beat 2 — The Feather Tickle Trigger

The thrown object is the soft plush toy. The beat name adds no feather.

**Start.** Beat 1 has ended. The kids are on the back side of the checkpoint line. The teacher is sleeping at the desk. One kid holds the soft plush. The designated shield mat is the one named before beat 1.

**Kids.** If the teacher starts to wake, that kid can throw the soft plush onto the mat. **Recommendation.** While the teacher is still starting to wake and the plush is not yet on the mat, that kid may throw again. A throw that misses the mat does not start the laugh.

If the plush lands on the mat, the kids may move for the 5-second laugh. **Requirement.** They still do this beat before the checkpoint and before the desk. **Recommendation.** During those 5 seconds they may change places and they stay on the back side of the line, with the prize unclaimed. The plush stays on the mat until the 5 seconds finish.

**Teacher.** May start to wake, still at the desk, eyes closed. That stir is the throw window. If the plush is on the mat, the teacher laughs uncontrollably for 5 seconds, at the desk. **DESIGN-DECISION.** Those 5 seconds are the movement window for the kids. **Recommendation.** The teacher stays seated and laughing for those 5 seconds, then sleeps again, still guarding the prize.

The teacher may sleep through the whole beat. **Requirement.** The session does not add a start-to-wake in order to make the throw happen.

If the teacher wakes up fully before the plush is on the mat, the throw window closes and every kid freezes in a pose. **Recommendation.** When the teacher sleeps again, beat 2 ends with no laugh.

**End.** If the plush landed, the beat ends when the 5-second laugh ends and the teacher is sleeping again. **Recommendation.** If the teacher never starts to wake, the teacher ends the beat with no throw and no laugh, and beat 3 starts. That exit is not in the requirements. See section 6.

**Mistake.** **Requirement.** Beat 2 states none. **Recommendation.** A miss, a closed window, or a beat with no start-to-wake leaves the kids where they are. Nobody takes steps back. The prize stays unclaimed.

### Beat 3 — The Copycat Pose

**Start.** Beat 2 has ended. The teacher is sleeping at the desk. The kids are on the back side of the checkpoint line. This beat adds no prop.

**Kids.** When the teacher suddenly opens their eyes, anyone who is moving freezes at once and imitates the statue. **Recommendation.** The statue they hold is the source example: balancing on one foot with a finger on the nose. A kid who is already still stays still and is not caught.

**Teacher.** Suddenly opens their eyes. **DESIGN-DECISION.** This is the copycat moment. It is separate from “starts to wake” and from “wakes up.” **Recommendation.** The teacher uses this eyes-open moment once in this beat. A full wake, if the teacher chooses one outside the laugh, is still the pose freeze for every kid, including during this beat before the eyes open.

**End.** **Recommendation.** The beat ends when the teacher closes their eyes and is sleeping again, and every kid who was caught moving has been a statue. If nobody was moving when the eyes opened, nobody is caught, and the beat still ends when the eyes close. Beat 4 starts. The requirements do not state this ending. See section 6.

**Mistake.** **Requirement.** If a kid who was caught moving laughs, that kid takes three steps back. That is the only send-back in the session. **Recommendation.** Each step is one ordinary footstep toward the back of the room. The kid takes the three steps when they laugh, stays at the new spot, and remains in the session. Nothing further is added. A kid who was not caught, and who laughs, stays put. After the eyes close, a later laugh does not cost steps.

### Beat 4 — The Sticky Note Sabotage

**Start.** Beat 3 has ended. The teacher is sleeping. The kids are on the back side of the line. The line is still unpassed. The sticky note shows a mustache or a goofy face. “Funny” is source tone. The teacher does not grade the drawing.

**Kids.** One kid sneaks up to the teacher and sticks that note on the teacher’s back. **Recommendation.** The teacher asks one kid to go. The other kids stay on the back side of the line until the pass counts.

**Teacher.** Stays sleeping at the desk and guarding the prize. The pass counts only if the teacher does not notice the note.

**End.** **Requirement.** The beat ends when the note is on the teacher’s back and the teacher has not noticed it. That is what passes the line. Until then the line stays unpassed. **Recommendation.** That one unnoticed note lets the whole group cross the line. Beat 5 starts with the group on the desk side of the line.

**Mistake.** **Requirement.** Beat 4 states none. Noticing the note has no written result. **Recommendation.** If the teacher notices, the teacher shows that they noticed, then sleeps again. That note does not pass the line. The kids stay where they are. Nobody takes steps back. The beat remains in progress until a note is on the back and unnoticed. A wake during the sneak freezes every kid in a pose until the teacher sleeps again. The sneak then continues.

### Beat 5 — The Confetti Victory Blast

**Start.** The checkpoint line has been passed. The kids are on the desk side of the line. The teacher is sleeping at the desk, guarding the prize. The blast chosen before beat 1 is at the desk.

**Kids.** One kid goes to the teacher’s desk. **Recommendation.** The teacher asks one kid to go. If that kid reaches the desk without getting caught, they claim the target prize and, as they claim it, do the one chosen blast.

- Safe party popper: they pop it.
- Paper confetti: they throw one handful over the teacher’s head.

**Recommendation.** “Caught” on this approach means the teacher wakes up before the claim, or opens their eyes while that kid is still moving, before the claim. A wake freezes every kid in a pose. Eyes open make a moving kid hold the one-foot statue. Either one means the claim has not happened.

**Teacher.** Sleeps at the desk unless they wake up or open their eyes, as above. **Recommendation.** They stay in the chair. They do not stand up during the claim.

**End.** **Requirement.** Phase A ends when a kid claims the target prize with the confetti victory blast. Either blast action is that blast. Reaching the desk is this beat. **DESIGN-DECISION.** The claim ends Phase A. Beat 10 still wins Phase B only, and only later. The session is won only after both finales. The props and the room stay.

**Mistake.** **Requirement.** Beat 5 states no failure result. The claim is written only for a kid who reaches the desk without getting caught, and Phase A ends only when that claim includes the blast. **Recommendation.** If the kid is caught, the prize stays on the desk, the blast has not been used, and nobody takes steps back. The beat remains in progress. When the teacher is sleeping again, a kid may go for the desk again from the desk side of the line. If the kid takes the prize and the blast has not happened with that claim, Phase A is still open until the claim and the blast happen together.

### What the host does when Phase A ends

Stop this script. Phase B starts immediately, in this same room, with this same teacher standing up and becoming the strict teacher. The kids’ path from the desk back to the back of the room is outside this slice.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

1. **Three wake moments.** “Starts to wake” is used only as beat 2’s throw window. “Wakes up” freezes every kid in a pose and may happen in any beat outside the 5-second laugh. “Opens their eyes” is beat 3’s statue for anyone still moving, and the recommended meaning of “caught” on the beat 5 approach. The 5-second laugh finishes before the eyes open.
2. **Laugh window.** For those 5 seconds the kids may move. Outside that window, a wake freezes them. The teacher stays at the desk laughing during the 5 seconds.
3. **The board.** Unused. This slice ends before Phase B.
4. **Both finales.** The beat 5 claim ends Phase A. It leaves the session unwinned. Beat 10 is still ahead and does not replace this phase.

## 5. Requirement fixes

None. The Phase A statements used here are playable once the recommendations above are labeled as recommendations. The missing beat 2 exit is already unknown 5, next to REQ-MEGA-010 and REQ-MEGA-042. It is an open fact, not a duplicate.

## 6. Open questions refused

These stay unanswered. A number, a measurement, a new joke, or a Phase B procedure would invent a requirement.

- How many kids are in the session.
- What “mid-way” measures, and the length of a step in any unit.
- Whether any statue besides one foot and a finger on the nose counts, and whether any sticky-note picture besides a mustache or a goofy face counts.
- How many sheets are in a handful, and whether a popped party popper travels over the teacher’s head.
- Which object counts as “a toy,” beyond the prize list.
- What “safe,” “soft,” and “sounds hilarious” require beyond those words.
- How long a pose or a statue lasts, in seconds. The only duration used is the 5-second laugh.
- How the kids get from the desk to the back of the room when Phase B opens.
- Whether Phase B props are already in the room during Phase A.
- Whether a later failure undoes a finished beat or a finished claim.
- A replay of a beat, a phase, or the session. An unfinished beat remaining in progress is the recommendation in section 4. A replay rule is not added.

**Beat 2 still cannot be finished from the requirements** when the teacher never starts to wake. REQ-MEGA-042 keeps the throw conditional. Unknown 5 leaves the exit open. The “end the beat with no throw” line in beat 2 is only a recommendation.

**Beat 1 still cannot be finished from the requirements.** The walk is required. The place where “across the room” ends, before beat 2, is unknown 3. Stopping on the back side of the line is only a recommendation.

**Beat 3 still cannot be finished from the requirements.** The statue and the three steps are required. Nothing states the moment the beat ends so beat 4 may start. Closing the eyes and sleeping again is only a recommendation.
