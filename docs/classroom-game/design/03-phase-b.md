# Phase B play — beats 6 through 10

A host runs this in one room, immediately after Phase A ends. Beats run in order: 6, 7, 8, 9, 10. Sentences marked **Requirement** are already in the core document. Sentences marked **RECOMMENDATION** are the smallest rule that lets one group play where that document leaves a gap. Sentences marked **DESIGN-DECISION** resolve a blocker the core document left open. They are not source requirements.

## 1. What this slice designs

Phase B, Game 2, Prank the Strict Teacher. Five beats, in this order:

| Beat | Name | What the kids are doing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | The Whoopee Cushion Pathway | Guessing which cushions to step on |
| 7 | The Reverse Homework Trick | Passing the clown sunglasses with their feet |
| 8 | The Doodle Attack | Drawing on the teacher’s shadow with a flashlight |
| 9 | The Monster Talk Distraction | One yell, then the others may crawl |
| 10 | The Silly String Finale | Note in the mailbox, gentle spray, victory dance |

This slice stages those beats so a host can start them, run the actions, and leave them. It uses the room and the props Phase A already left behind. It adds no new beat, no new prop, no score, no timer, no player count, and no send-back.

The phase goal sits under the beats. The kids deliver a secret note to the target mailbox on the other side of the room (REQ-MEGA-026, REQ-MEGA-058). Beat 10 is still last (REQ-MEGA-002). The finale starts once that note reaches the mailbox (REQ-MEGA-034).

## 2. The design

### How the host opens Phase B

Phase A has already ended: a kid claimed the target prize with the confetti victory blast (REQ-MEGA-056). Phase B starts immediately, in that same room (REQ-MEGA-020).

**Requirement.** The teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher (REQ-MEGA-021) and pretends to be very strict (REQ-MEGA-022). The strict teacher is standing facing a whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book. Any one of those three meets the requirement (REQ-MEGA-057).

**Requirement.** The kids are at the back of the room, including the kid who just claimed the prize at the teacher’s desk (REQ-MEGA-023). Props and the room from Phase A are still in the room (REQ-MEGA-024). The desk beat 10 may spray is that same teacher’s desk (REQ-MEGA-025).

**RECOMMENDATION.** The kids walk from the teacher’s desk to the back before anyone steps on a cushion. During that walk the host sets the Phase B objects:

- Cushions lining a path that starts at the back and runs toward the other side of the room.
- Exactly three whoopee cushions, hidden under exactly three of those floor cushions, one under each. The host hides them and does not tell the kids which three.
- The target mailbox on the other side of the room from the back, empty.
- One secret note, held by one kid the group chooses, at the back.
- The giant oversized pair of clown sunglasses, the flashlight, and the silly string, in the room and off the cushions.
- Any Phase A prop that was on the floor, moved off the cushion path and left in the room.

The host chooses the strict-teacher activity and keeps that same activity until beat 8. The host does not read required words off the secret note. Tone words such as “funny” are not a grade.

Beats 6–10 use the results written on those beats. The host does not apply Phase A’s freeze, statue, or three steps as a penalty on these beats.

### Beat 6 — The Whoopee Cushion Pathway

**Start.** The kids are at the back. The floor of the path is lined with cushions (REQ-MEGA-027). Exactly three of those cushions have hidden whoopee cushions underneath (REQ-MEGA-059). The secret note is still with the carrier. The mailbox is still empty.

**Actions.** The kids guess which cushions are safe to step on (REQ-MEGA-028). A guess is a step onto a cushion. One kid steps at a time, so the group can tell which step made a sound. The carrier keeps the note while stepping.

A step onto a cushion that hides a whoopee cushion is a wrong pick. That step makes a loud noise (REQ-MEGA-060). The noise is that whoopee cushion. “Funny” describes the noise. The host does not grade it.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** Beat 6 ends when every kid has stepped off the cushion path onto the floor on the mailbox side of the last cushion. The kids wait there. Beat 7 starts from that spot. The note is still with the carrier. The mailbox is still empty.

**Wrong pick.** **Requirement.** The step makes a loud noise (REQ-MEGA-060). That noise is the written result. It is not a send-back (REQ-MEGA-040, REQ-MEGA-071).

**RECOMMENDATION.** After the noise, that kid stays on the cushion they stepped on. The whoopee cushion stays under it. They may step to another cushion, including another noisy one. A step that makes no loud noise is treated the same way for movement: the kid is on that cushion and may step onward. The host does not send anyone to the back, does not remove a sounded whoopee cushion, and does not require a silent cushion to exist. The strict teacher stays in the activity chosen at the opening.

### Beat 7 — The Reverse Homework Trick

**Start.** Beat 6 has ended. The kids are together on the mailbox side of the cushion path. The note is still with the carrier.

**Actions.** The kids do this pass instead of doing work (REQ-MEGA-029). They pass one giant, oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line (REQ-MEGA-061), using only their feet (REQ-MEGA-062), without dropping the pair (REQ-MEGA-063).

**RECOMMENDATION.** The host does not hand out schoolwork. The kids stand in one line facing the mailbox. The line runs from the end farther from the mailbox to the end closer to it. “Down the line” is toward the mailbox. The host sets the sunglasses on the floor at the feet of the kid at the far end. That kid passes them with their feet to the next kid’s feet, and so on. Hands stay off the sunglasses during the pass. The carrier may be anywhere in the line and keeps the note.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** Beat 7 ends when the kid at the mailbox end of the line has the sunglasses, and every pass that got them there was made with feet. The pair is not lying on the floor from a drop. Beat 8 starts with the kids still in that line. The note is still with the carrier.

**If the pass breaks.** The requirements state no failure result (REQ-MEGA-039, REQ-MEGA-071).

**RECOMMENDATION.** A drop, or a pass that uses something other than feet, does not send anyone back and does not restart beats 6 or 7. The host sets the sunglasses on the floor at the feet of the last kid who held them with their feet. The pass continues from that kid, feet only. If no foot-pass has succeeded yet, they go back to the far end of the same line and start the pass again from those feet. Earlier beats stay finished.

### Beat 8 — The Doodle Attack

**Start.** Beat 7 has ended. The kids are in the sunglasses line. The strict teacher is still in the activity chosen at the opening. The note is still with the carrier.

**DESIGN-DECISION 3.** “The board” means the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing the whiteboard, the doodle window opens when they turn away and then turn back. If they are typing or reading, the doodle window opens when they turn to face the whiteboard.

**Actions.** The drawing happens when the teacher turns around to face the board (REQ-MEGA-030). No different signal replaces that turn (REQ-MEGA-043). The kids then race to the teacher’s shadow on the wall (REQ-MEGA-064). They draw with a flashlight (REQ-MEGA-065). The drawing is a hat, or giant ears. Any one of those meets the requirement (REQ-MEGA-066). “Funny” on the hat is tone. “Quickly” is not a counted time.

**RECOMMENDATION.** The host, playing the teacher, makes the turn in DESIGN-DECISION 3 once the kids are ready. The kids name one drawing before the beam touches the shadow: a hat, or giant ears. A kid carries the flashlight from the line to the shadow. The race is the group moving to that shadow together. There is no winner and no prize for arriving first. The kid with the flashlight draws the named picture by aiming the beam at the teacher’s shadow so the lit shape reads as that hat or those giant ears. The host has the teacher’s shadow visible on one wall while the teacher faces the whiteboard. The flashlight is the drawing tool.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** Beat 8 ends when that one drawing is on the shadow and the teacher has faced the whiteboard for the window in which it was drawn. The kids stay at the shadow. Beat 9 starts there. The note is still with the carrier.

The requirements give this beat no other exit. If the teacher never turns to face the board, the drawing waits (REQ-MEGA-030, REQ-MEGA-043). **RECOMMENDATION.** The host makes that turn. The kids do not invent a signal.

**If the doodle misses.** The requirements state no failure result (REQ-MEGA-039, REQ-MEGA-071).

**RECOMMENDATION.** A beam that misses the shadow, or a shape that is not the named hat or the named giant ears, does not send anyone back. The kids aim at the shadow and draw the named picture. If the teacher has turned away before that picture is on the shadow, the window closes. The kids wait until the teacher turns to face the whiteboard again, by the same turn as DESIGN-DECISION 3. They do not use a clap, a word, or any other signal. String, cushions, and the note stay as they are. Beats 6 and 7 stay finished.

### Beat 9 — The Monster Talk Distraction

**Start.** Beat 8 has ended. The kids are at the shadow. The note is still with the carrier. The mailbox is still empty.

**Actions.** To move forward, one kid yells a phrase in a deep monster voice (REQ-MEGA-031). “The broccoli has landed!” is a source example of that phrase (REQ-MEGA-032). On that yell, the teacher turns around to investigate the sound (REQ-MEGA-033). “Silly,” “goofy,” and “confused” are tone. The host does not grade the voice or the teacher’s face. While the teacher is turned to investigate, the other kids can crawl forward (REQ-MEGA-067).

**RECOMMENDATION.** The group chooses the one kid who yells. That kid yells the example phrase, “The broccoli has landed!”, in a deep monster voice. The host then turns the teacher around to investigate. The other kids may crawl forward, toward the mailbox, as far as they choose on that crawl. The carrier crawls with the note if they are one of the other kids, and keeps hold of it.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** Beat 9 ends when the teacher has turned to investigate that yell. The crawl may happen before the host starts beat 10. The beat still ends if nobody crawls. Beat 10 starts with the kids where that crawl, or the lack of one, left them. The note is still with the carrier. The mailbox is still empty.

**If the yell or the crawl goes wrong.** The requirements state no failure result (REQ-MEGA-039, REQ-MEGA-071). The crawl is written as something the other kids can do. This design does not turn it into a gate and does not forbid the yelling kid to crawl.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Nobody moves back. If the chosen kid has not yet yelled “The broccoli has landed!” in a deep monster voice, that kid does so, and the teacher then turns. A crawl by the yelling kid, or a forward move that is not a crawl, does not send anyone back and does not undo beat 8. The host still leaves the beat on the teacher’s turn. Whether those extra movements count as the beat’s forward move stays open in section 6.

### Beat 10 — The Silly String Finale

**Start.** Beats 6, 7, 8, and 9 have ended, in that order. The carrier still has the secret note. The mailbox is on the other side of the room and is empty.

**Requirement.** The finale starts once the secret note reaches the target mailbox (REQ-MEGA-034).

**RECOMMENDATION.** The carrier puts the note into the mailbox now. That is the first action of beat 10. The note does not go into the mailbox during beats 6–9.

**Actions.** The kids gently spray silly string (REQ-MEGA-068). The spray meets the requirement if it hits the teacher’s desk, or the teacher’s shoes. Any one of those two (REQ-MEGA-069). The desk is the teacher’s desk carried from Phase A (REQ-MEGA-025). While they spray, the kids do a victory dance (REQ-MEGA-070).

**RECOMMENDATION.** Before the spray, the kids name one target: the teacher’s desk, or the teacher’s shoes. One kid carries the silly string to that target and sprays it once, gently, so the string lands on the named target. The kids dance in place for as long as that spray lasts. The host does not call out dance steps. The host does not measure the spray.

**End.** **Requirement.** Phase B ends with the silly string finale (REQ-MEGA-035). The finale is that gentle spray and the victory dance, begun once the note is in the mailbox.

**DESIGN-DECISION 4.** Completing this finale wins Phase B. The session is won only when the confetti victory blast and the silly string finale have both happened (REQ-MEGA-036). “Win the game” in beat 10 is this Phase B win. It does not skip Phase A. In a session played in order, the confetti victory blast has already happened, so the host may announce the session win only after this finale is complete. The note landing in the mailbox starts the finale. It does not, by itself, win Phase B or the session.

**If the spray or the dance is off.** The requirements state no failure result (REQ-MEGA-039, REQ-MEGA-071).

**RECOMMENDATION.** A spray that is not gentle, a spray that hits anything other than the one named target, or a spray with no victory dance during it, leaves the finale unfinished. Nobody goes back to beat 6, 7, 8, or 9. String that already landed stays where it landed. The kids then do one gentle spray on the named target, and they dance while that spray happens. Phase B ends when that spray and that dance happen together. The host does not clear the desk, the shoes, or the mailbox.

### Host card

1. Phase A finale is done. Kids walk to the back. Teacher stands and plays one strict activity. Host hides exactly three whoopee cushions. Mailbox is empty. One kid holds the note.
2. Beat 6. Kids step and guess. A whoopee step is loud. They keep going. End when everyone is off the path on the mailbox side.
3. Beat 7. Foot-pass the sunglasses down the line. End when the last kid has them.
4. Beat 8. Teacher turns to face the whiteboard, by DESIGN-DECISION 3. Kids race to the shadow and flashlight-draw a hat or giant ears. End when that drawing is on the shadow.
5. Beat 9. One kid yells “The broccoli has landed!” in a deep monster voice. Teacher turns to investigate. The other kids may crawl forward. End on that turn.
6. Beat 10. Carrier puts the note in the mailbox. Kids gently spray the desk or the shoes and dance while they spray. Phase B ends. Phase B is won. The session is won only because the Phase A finale already happened as well.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

**DESIGN-DECISION 3, used in beat 8.** “The board” means the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the doodle window opens when they turn away and then turn back. If they are typing or reading, the doodle window opens when they turn to face the whiteboard.

This is how the host produces the turn in REQ-MEGA-030. It does not add a second board. It does not replace the turn with a different signal (REQ-MEGA-043).

**DESIGN-DECISION 4, used in beat 10.** Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales. “Win the game” in beat 10 does not skip Phase A.

The Phase B win is the completed silly string finale: the note is in the mailbox, the spray is gentle, the spray is on the one named target, and the kids dance while they spray. The mailbox moment starts that finale (REQ-MEGA-034). The phase ends with the finale (REQ-MEGA-035). The session win remains REQ-MEGA-036.

DESIGN-DECISION 1 and DESIGN-DECISION 2 are the Phase A wake moments and the 5-second laugh. This slice does not use them. Beats 6–10 follow the results written on those beats.

## 6. Open questions this design refused to guess

These stay open. A host can run the five beats with the recommendations above and still leave every item on this list undecided.

- How many kids are in the session. This design sets no count. With one kid, the line pass and “the other kids can crawl” have no second kid. This design does not invent a solo form of beat 7 or beat 9.
- Whether any monster phrase besides “The broccoli has landed!” counts.
- Whether one shadow drawing may show both a hat and giant ears.
- Whether one finale may spray both the teacher’s desk and the teacher’s shoes.
- What the secret note says.
- Which movements count as the victory dance, and whether a kid who is spraying must dance with the same movements as the others.
- What “gentle” requires beyond that word. What a “safe” cushion requires beyond the guess and the loud wrong step.
- How long the teacher faces the whiteboard. How long the investigation lasts. How far one crawl goes.
- What casts the teacher’s shadow, beyond the host making that shadow visible on one wall.
- Whether the yelling kid may crawl. Whether the other kids may move forward by any movement other than crawling. Whether beat 9’s forward move succeeds when they do not crawl. The end recommendation lets the host start beat 10 anyway.
- The teacher’s reaction to a whoopee noise, the doodle, the yell, and the silly string, and what the teacher does after Phase B ends.
- Measured positions for the mailbox, the whiteboard, the computer, the big book, and the cushion path.
- How many cushions line the floor, and which three hide the whoopee cushions.
- Whether a Phase A prop has a job during beats 6–10 beyond remaining in the room.
- Whether a later mistake undoes a finished beat, a note already in the mailbox, or a finale that has already ended.
- Replay of a beat, a phase, or the session, and whether the group may pick the hat, the ears, the desk, or the shoes again on a later session.
- Whether the Phase B props were already in the room during Phase A.

### Beats the requirements do not finish

A host can run every beat with the recommendations in section 4. The requirements alone still do not close these:

- **Beat 6.** The wrong step has a written result, the loud noise. Nothing in the requirements says when the pathway is over or where the kids stand after the noise.
- **Beat 7.** Nothing in the requirements says when the pass is over, where a dropped pair returns, or what happens when the pass uses something other than feet.
- **Beat 8.** The turn, the race, and the drawing are written. Nothing in the requirements says the beat is over when the drawing is done. If the teacher never turns to face the board, the requirements leave no exit.
- **Beat 9.** The yell, the teacher’s turn, and the optional crawl are written. Nothing in the requirements says when the beat is over, or that the beat succeeds when nobody crawls.
- **Beat 10, success path.** The requirements do finish this path. The finale starts when the note reaches the mailbox, the kids gently spray the desk or the shoes while they dance, and Phase B ends with that finale. The session is won only when the confetti victory blast has happened as well. The Phase B win is DESIGN-DECISION 4, not a requirement.
- **Beat 10, off path.** Nothing in the requirements says what happens when the spray is not gentle, hits something other than the desk or the shoes, or happens with no victory dance.

Beats 7, 8, 9, and 10 state no failure result. Beat 6’s noise is a result, and it is not a failure that ends the beat. This design recommends continuation. It does not add a failure rule.
