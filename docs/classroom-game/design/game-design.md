# Game design

One room. One teacher. One session. Phase A is beats 1–5. Phase B is beats 6–10 in the same room. A line tagged **Requirement** is already in the core document. A line tagged **DESIGN-DECISION** is one of the four decisions in the design brief. A line tagged **RECOMMENDATION** is a rule a design slice already gave for a gap the requirements leave open. It is not a requirement.

## 1. What this slice designs

The sheet a host can run from. Roles, the five picks, the room, the teacher’s states, a start and an end for each of the ten beats, the only written send-back, both finales, and the session win.

No new beat, prop, image, or punishment. Where the slices disagree, this sheet uses the exit that lets the written beat finish and keeps the only send-back on beat 3.

## 2. The design

### Roles

Two roles. The same people stay in them for both phases.

**Teacher.** One person, in the room the whole session. Asleep at the desk in Phase A. Standing as the strict teacher in Phase B. **RECOMMENDATION.** The teacher is the host. The teacher calls the beats and the states, then returns to the scene. No third role.

**Kids.** One shared role on one shared track. They sneak for the target prize in Phase A. They deliver the secret note in Phase B. They do not split into teams. The session has no score.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Two kids is the smallest group that can pass the sunglasses down a line and still have one kid yell while another kid crawls. Extra kids join the same kid role. Any kid may do an action written for “a kid.” An action written for “the kids” is the group. One claim with the blast ends Phase A for the whole group. The others do not each claim a prize, and they do not each have to clear beats 1–4 on their own before that claim.

### The five host picks

The host makes all five before Phase A. Each pick is one option. The unchosen partner stays out of that job. **RECOMMENDATION.** Each pick lasts through both finales.

| Pick | Choose one | Where it is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prize type | A golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats | The one target prize on the desk. A kid claims it on beat 5. |
| Shoe noisemaker | A squeaky dog toy, or bubble wrap | Under the feet on the beat 1 walk. |
| Victory effect | Pop a safe party popper, or throw a handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head | The blast that ends Phase A. |
| Strict-teacher activity | Standing facing the whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book | From the Phase B stand-up until the doodle turn. |
| Silly-string target | The teacher’s desk, or the teacher’s shoes | The spray in beat 10. |

**RECOMMENDATION.** If the prize is “a toy,” the host shows one object and names it as the prize. That object is not the plush, the sunglasses, or a whoopee cushion. Every kid on the beat 1 walk uses the one noisemaker kind. One handful of confetti is what one kid can hold in one hand. The popper, if that is the blast, is popped at the desk. “Over the teacher’s head” belongs to the confetti throw.

The hat or the giant ears is a real choice inside beat 8. It is not a sixth pick before the session. **RECOMMENDATION.** The kids name one of those two drawings before the flashlight beam touches the shadow.

Three samples stay examples. **RECOMMENDATION.** If the host names nothing, the group uses the sample. The host may name a replacement once, before the beat that uses it. The host does not grade “funny,” “goofy,” or “silly.”

| Sample | Used when |
| --- | --- |
| Balancing on one foot with a finger on the nose | Eyes open, for a kid who is still moving |
| A mustache, or a goofy face, on the sticky note | Beat 4 |
| “The broccoli has landed!” | Beat 9, in a deep monster voice |

### The room

One classroom. The back is where the kids start. The front is the desk. The teacher’s desk is at the front. The mailbox is on the other side of the room from the back, and it is not on the desk. The whiteboard is the board. **DESIGN-DECISION.** There is one board.

**RECOMMENDATION.** The host places the props before the kids play, and hides the three whoopee cushions without saying which three.

- **Open path.** Clear floor from the back to the front edge of the desk. Phase A walks this path.
- **Checkpoint line.** The host marks the existing line by eye across the open path, between the back and the desk. Nothing is measured. Beat 1 stops on the back side. Beat 4 crosses it. It does not gate Phase B.
- **Cushion line.** A file of cushions along the other side of the floor, from the back toward the mailbox, clear of the open path and the mat. Three of those cushions hide one whoopee cushion each. **RECOMMENDATION.** The host may lay more cushions than three, so a quiet step can exist. A quiet cushion is not required for the beat to end.
- **Shield mat.** Beside the desk, off both paths, where a kid still on the back side of the line can throw the plush onto it. The host points to it and names it before beat 1.
- **Desktop.** The locked prize nearest the class. The computer and the big book sit on the desk. The teacher sleeps over the prize. Shoes stay on the teacher’s feet all session.
- **Back cluster.** Off the open path: the plush, the sticky note, the locked noisemaker, the locked blast, the secret note, the sunglasses, the flashlight, and the silly string. Phase B hand props wait there through Phase A.

The beat title The Feather Tickle Trigger adds no feather. The thrown object is the soft plush. Beat 3 adds no prop. Beat 9 adds no prop.

### Teacher states

The host says the state and performs it. Kid noise does not force a state. The plush on the mat forces the laugh. The monster yell turns the teacher to investigate. The only counted time is the 5-second laugh.

**DESIGN-DECISION.** “Starts to wake,” “Wakes up,” and “Opens their eyes” are three states. The host uses one at a time. “Starts to wake” is only beat 2.

**DESIGN-DECISION.** During the 5-second laugh, kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them. The host does not call “Wakes up” or “Eyes open” during the five seconds.

| State | When | Teacher | Kids |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asleep | Phase A, whenever no other Phase A state is called | Sleep at the desk, guarding the prize | Sneak, walk, throw, note, and claim, on the beat that allows it |
| Starting to wake | Beat 2 only | Stir at the desk, eyes closed. This is the throw window | One kid may throw the plush onto the mat. Nobody freezes |
| Laughing | The plush is on the mat | Laugh for 5 seconds, seated, then sleep again | Move for those 5 seconds. **RECOMMENDATION.** They stay on the back side of the line. The prize stays unclaimed |
| Wakes up | Phase A, while Asleep, outside the laugh. The host may skip this call | Wake at the desk so the room can see | Every kid freezes in a still pose. **RECOMMENDATION.** Any still pose. The host does not grade it. It lasts until the host says Asleep. Then the current beat continues. No steps |
| Eyes open | Beat 3, once, outside the laugh | Open the eyes suddenly | Anyone still moving freezes into the statue. A kid who was already still is not caught |
| Standing into Phase B | The claim with the blast has just happened | Stand and become the strict teacher | Walk to the back. No freeze and no steps |
| Strict activity | One pick: facing the whiteboard, typing, or reading | Pretend to be very strict in that activity | Beats 6 and 7. The whoopee and the sunglasses do not change the activity |
| Doodle turn | Beat 8 | See the turn under beat 8 | Race to the shadow and draw |
| Investigating | Beat 9, after the yell | Turn away and investigate the sound | The other kids may crawl toward the mailbox |
| Silly string | Beat 10 | Stay in the strict activity. No extra reaction | Spray and dance |

**RECOMMENDATION.** The teacher stays at the desk through all of Phase A, including the laugh. Standing up is the Phase B start. Phase A’s pose, statue, and three steps stop when the teacher stands. Phase B does not use them.

### The only send-back

**Requirement.** If a kid who was caught moving laughs, that kid takes three steps back. This is the only send-back in the session. It belongs to Eyes open. A laugh during the 5-second window is not this laugh. A laugh from a kid who was not caught is not this laugh. A laugh during a wake pose is not this laugh.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Caught, at Eyes open, means the kid was moving when the eyes opened. Back faces the back of the room. Each step is one footfall of that kid. The three steps start when that kid laughs. After the third step, the kid stays there and continues the session from that spot. Other kids stay where they are. A note already unnoticed stays on the back. A prize already claimed stays claimed. A beat the group has already left stays finished. If the kid is already against the back wall, the remaining steps are steps in place.

No other beat sends anyone back. A miss, a noise, a drop, a noticed note, or a skipped crawl leaves the kids where they are. The beat stays in progress until its end below.

### Beat 1 — The Squeaky Toy Shoes

**Start.** The teacher is asleep at the desk, guarding the prize. The kids are at the back. Each walker has the chosen noisemaker.

**Play.** **Requirement.** The kids walk from the back with that object under their feet. Silent sneaking is impossible. The sound is the object. Finishing the walk does not claim the prize. If the teacher calls Wakes up, every kid freezes until the teacher is asleep again, and the walk then continues.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** The walk ends when every kid who is walking has walked with the object under their feet and the group is still on the back side of the checkpoint line. The objects come out from under the feet. Beat 2 starts. A kid with nothing under a foot puts the chosen object under that foot and walks. Nobody takes steps back.

### Beat 2 — The Feather Tickle Trigger

**Start.** Beat 1 has ended. The kids are on the back side of the line. The teacher is asleep. One kid holds the plush. The mat is the one named before beat 1.

**Play.** **Requirement.** If the teacher starts to wake, a kid can throw the soft plush onto the mat. A plush on the mat forces a 5-second laugh, and the kids may move for those 5 seconds. The session does not add a wake to force the throw. **RECOMMENDATION.** During that same stir, a miss may be thrown again. A miss does not start the laugh. The plush stays on the mat through the 5 seconds. The teacher stays seated and laughing, then sleeps again, still guarding the prize.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** If the plush landed, the beat ends when the 5 seconds end and the teacher is asleep again. If the teacher never starts to wake, the host gives one quiet pause and ends the beat with no throw and no laugh. If the teacher wakes fully before the plush is on the mat, the throw window closes, the kids freeze, and the beat ends with no laugh when the teacher is asleep again. Beat 3 starts from where the kids are. Nobody takes steps back.

### Beat 3 — The Copycat Pose

**Start.** Beat 2 has ended. The teacher is asleep. The kids are on the back side of the line. This beat has no prop.

**Play.** **Requirement.** The teacher opens their eyes. Anyone caught moving freezes and imitates a statue. **DESIGN-DECISION.** This is the copycat moment, separate from “starts to wake” and from “wakes up.” **RECOMMENDATION.** The statue is the one-foot sample, unless the host named a replacement. A kid who was already still stays still and is not caught. The host uses Eyes open once in this beat.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** The beat ends when the eyes have opened once, every kid who was moving has been a statue, any caught kid who laughed has taken the three steps, and the teacher is asleep again. If nobody was moving, nobody is caught, and the beat still ends when the eyes close. Beat 4 starts.

### Beat 4 — The Sticky Note Sabotage

**Start.** Beat 3 has ended. The teacher is asleep. The kids are on the back side of the line. The line is unpassed. The note shows the sample picture, or the host’s replacement.

**Play.** **Requirement.** Passing the line takes one kid sneaking to the teacher, a sticky note on the teacher’s back, and the teacher not noticing. **RECOMMENDATION.** The host asks one kid to go. The other kids stay on the back side until the pass counts. The teacher plays the note as unnoticed.

**End.** **Requirement.** The pass counts when the note is on the back and the teacher has not noticed it. **RECOMMENDATION.** That one note lets the whole group cross to the desk side of the line. Beat 5 starts there. If the teacher notices, that note does not pass the line. The teacher settles back to sleep. The kids stay where they are, and a kid may try again. Nobody takes steps back. The beat stays open until one note is unnoticed and the group has crossed.

### Beat 5 — The Confetti Victory Blast

**Start.** The line has been passed. The kids are on the desk side. The teacher is asleep, guarding the prize. The chosen blast is at the desk.

**Play.** **Requirement.** A kid who reaches the desk without getting caught claims the target prize and does the confetti victory blast. The blast is the one pick: pop the safe party popper, or throw the handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head. **RECOMMENDATION.** On this approach, caught means Eyes open finds that kid moving before the claim. A wake pose pauses every kid until the teacher is asleep again. A finished pose or a finished statue does not bar a later arrival. The teacher stays in the chair. If the kid is caught, the prize stays on the desk, the blast stays unused, and a kid may go again from the desk side once the teacher is asleep. Nobody takes steps back for the miss.

**End.** **Requirement.** Phase A ends when that kid claims the prize with the blast. Reaching the desk is this beat. The session is still open.

### The join

This is not a beat.

**Start.** The blast has just ended. The claiming kid is at the desk.

**Play.** **Requirement.** Phase B starts in this same room. The teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher, in the activity already picked. The kids, including the kid who claimed the prize, are at the back before beat 6. Props from Phase A stay. **RECOMMENDATION.** The kids walk the open path to the back. The claimed prize stays with the kid who claimed it. One kid takes the secret note from the back cluster and carries it from here through beat 9. The flashlight and the silly string may be picked up too. The walk has no freeze and no send-back.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** The join ends when the kids are at the back and the teacher is standing in the chosen activity. Beat 6 starts.

### Beat 6 — The Whoopee Cushion Pathway

**Start.** The kids are at the back. The cushion line is on the floor. Three cushions hide a whoopee cushion. The carrier still has the note. The mailbox is empty.

**Play.** **Requirement.** The kids guess which cushions are safe. A wrong step makes a loud noise. The noise is the result this beat writes. It is not a send-back. **RECOMMENDATION.** A guess is one kid stepping on one cushion. After the noise, that kid stays on that cushion and may step onward. The whoopee stays under it. A quiet step works the same way for movement. The teacher stays in the chosen activity. The carrier keeps the note.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** The beat ends when every kid has stepped off the cushion path onto the floor on the mailbox side. The kids wait there. The note is still with the carrier. The mailbox is still empty. Beat 7 starts from that spot.

### Beat 7 — The Reverse Homework Trick

**Start.** Beat 6 has ended. The kids are together on the mailbox side of the cushions. The carrier still has the note.

**Play.** **Requirement.** The kids pass the giant clown sunglasses down the line, using only their feet, without dropping them. This pass is what they do instead of work. **RECOMMENDATION.** The host does not hand out schoolwork. The kids stand in one line. The line runs from the end farther from the mailbox toward the mailbox. The sunglasses start on the floor at the feet of the kid at the far end. Hands stay off the pair. The note, if this kid is carrying it, stays in a hand. If the pair drops, or a pass uses something other than a foot, the pair goes back to the feet of the last kid who held it with their feet. The pass continues from that kid. Bodies stay in line. Earlier beats stay finished.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** The beat ends when the kid at the mailbox end has the sunglasses, and every pass that got them there was made with feet. The pair is not lying on the floor from a drop. Beat 8 starts with the kids still in that line. The note is still with the carrier.

### Beat 8 — The Doodle Attack

**Start.** Beat 7 has ended. The kids are in the sunglasses line. The teacher is still in the activity picked before the session. The carrier still has the note.

**Play.** **DESIGN-DECISION.** The board is the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the doodle window opens when they turn away and then turn back. If they are typing or reading, the window opens when they turn to face the whiteboard. **Requirement.** The kids race to the teacher’s shadow on the wall and draw with the flashlight. The drawing is a hat, or giant ears. No other signal replaces that turn. **RECOMMENDATION.** The host makes that turn. The kids name one drawing. The race is the group moving to the shadow. The kid with the flashlight draws that one picture on the shadow. Ordinary room light casts the shadow on the wall the teacher is facing. The flashlight is the drawing tool.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** The beat ends when that one drawing is on the shadow. The kids stay at the shadow. If the teacher never makes the turn, the host gives one quiet pause and then ends the beat with no drawing. The kids do not invent a signal. Nobody takes steps back. Beat 9 starts. The note is still with the carrier.

### Beat 9 — The Monster Talk Distraction

**Start.** Beat 8 has ended. The kids are where beat 8 left them. The carrier still has the note. The mailbox is empty.

**Play.** **Requirement.** One kid yells a phrase in a deep monster voice. On that yell, the teacher turns to investigate. While the teacher is turned, the other kids can crawl forward. **RECOMMENDATION.** The yell is the sample phrase, unless the host named a replacement. The other kids may crawl toward the mailbox. The yelling kid’s action is the yell. The carrier crawls with the note if they are one of the other kids. A crawl is allowed. It is not required.

**End.** **RECOMMENDATION.** The beat ends when the teacher has turned to investigate and then turned back. The other kids have had a chance to crawl. Zero crawls still ends the beat. The kids keep the ground a crawl gained. The note is still with the carrier. The mailbox is still empty. If the note goes into the mailbox early, the host hands it back to the carrier. The finale waits. Beat 10 starts. Nobody takes steps back.

### Beat 10 — The Silly String Finale

**Start.** Beats 6, 7, 8, and 9 have ended, in that order. The carrier still has the secret note. The mailbox is empty.

**Play.** **Requirement.** The finale starts once the secret note reaches the target mailbox. The kids gently spray silly string on the locked target, the teacher’s desk or the teacher’s shoes, and they do a victory dance while they spray. The desk is the desk from Phase A. **RECOMMENDATION.** The carrier puts the note into the mailbox now. That is the first action of this beat. One kid sprays that one target once, gently. The kids dance in place for as long as the spray lasts. The host does not call dance steps and does not measure the spray. String that lands stays where it landed. If the spray misses the locked target, or the dance does not happen during it, the kids spray that target once more and dance while that spray happens. Nobody returns to an earlier beat.

**End.** **Requirement.** Phase B ends with that finale: the note is in the mailbox, the spray is on the one target, and the kids dance while they spray.

### Both finales and the session win

**Requirement.** The confetti victory blast ends Phase A. It does not win the session. The silly string finale ends Phase B.

**DESIGN-DECISION.** Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales. “Win the game” in beat 10 does not skip Phase A.

In a session played in order, the blast has already happened when the spray and the dance finish. The host then says the session is won, and play stops. The note landing in the mailbox starts the finale. It does not, by itself, win Phase B or the session. A finale that has happened stays happened. The teacher has no separate win.

### Run order

1. Lock the five picks. Set the room. Teacher asleep at the desk. Kids at the back.
2. Beat 1. Noisy walk. Stop on the back side of the line.
3. Beat 2. Start-to-wake window. A hit is the 5-second laugh, then sleep. No hit still ends the beat.
4. Beat 3. Eyes open. Movers statue. A caught kid who laughs takes three steps back. Then sleep.
5. Beat 4. One unnoticed note. The group crosses the line.
6. Beat 5. One uncaught kid claims the prize and does the one blast. Phase A ends.
7. Kids walk to the back. Teacher stands into the chosen activity. One kid holds the note.
8. Beat 6. Step the cushions. A whoopee is noise. End on the mailbox side of the path.
9. Beat 7. Foot-pass the sunglasses toward the mailbox. End when the last kid has them.
10. Beat 8. The whiteboard turn. One drawing on the shadow, or the quiet pause if the turn never comes.
11. Beat 9. One yell. Teacher looks, then turns back. Others may crawl.
12. Beat 10. Note in the mailbox. Gentle spray on the locked target. Dance while spraying. Phase B is won. The session is won.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

**DESIGN-DECISION 1.** Three wake moments. “Starts to wake” is only the feather-trigger window, and that window is beat 2. “Wakes up” freezes every kid in a ridiculous pose. “Opens their eyes” is the copycat statue for anyone still moving. Used in the state table and in beats 2, 3, and 5. The pose is any still pose. The statue is the written sample unless the host replaced it. No feather prop.

**DESIGN-DECISION 2.** During the 5-second laugh, kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them. Used in the laugh state and in beat 2. The teacher uses those 5 seconds to laugh. When they end, the teacher is asleep again. The end of the count is not a new wake.

**DESIGN-DECISION 3.** “The board” means the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the doodle window opens when they turn away and then turn back. If they are typing or reading, the doodle window opens when they turn to face the whiteboard. Used in beat 8. One board. No replacement signal.

**DESIGN-DECISION 4.** Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales. “Win the game” in beat 10 does not skip Phase A. Used in the finales. The Phase B win is the finished silly string finale. The session win is that finale plus the blast, which has already happened.

## 5. Requirement fixes

The core document was not edited. These are the duplicates still in it.

1. **REQ-MEGA-012 and REQ-MEGA-050.** One eyes-open reaction, two ids. The moving kid freezes, and that freeze is the statue.
2. **REQ-MEGA-038, REQ-MEGA-040, and the sentence after REQ-MEGA-077.** One send-back policy, three writings. Only beat 3’s three steps send anyone back. REQ-MEGA-014 is the step rule itself, so it is not part of this copy.
3. **REQ-MEGA-039 and REQ-MEGA-071.** One lock, two writings. A beat with no failure rule has no failure result, and a later pass does not add one.
4. **REQ-MEGA-054, REQ-MEGA-055, and the action list inside REQ-MEGA-019.** The popper and the confetti are defined, then named again as the blast. The words that are not a copy are “any one of these is the confetti victory blast.”
5. **REQ-MEGA-015.** “Both sentences stay” repeats the fact that REQ-MEGA-007 and REQ-MEGA-012 are already in the list.
6. **REQ-MEGA-047 beside REQ-MEGA-006 and REQ-MEGA-018.** Start at the back, and take the prize, are already those two requirements plus the beat order. REQ-MEGA-047 states the grab with no beat number.
7. **Examples table, sticky note.** Section 5 joins the mustache and the goofy face with “or,” the same join the core uses for a real choice. REQ-MEGA-017 calls both drawings examples introduced with “like.”

## 6. Open questions refused

- A required headcount above the recommended two, and any age, grade, or room measurement.
- A timer besides the 5-second laugh.
- Any send-back besides the three steps.
- The words on the secret note, the steps of the victory dance, and a new joke.
- What “safe,” “soft,” and “gentle” require beyond those words.
- Whether one drawing may show both a hat and giant ears.
- Whether the popper must travel over the teacher’s head, and how many sheets are in a handful.
- A solo form of the line pass or of “the other kids can crawl.”
- A replay, or a win for the teacher.
