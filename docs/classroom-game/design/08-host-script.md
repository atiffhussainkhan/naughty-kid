# Slice 08 — Host script

## 1. What this slice designs

The words one host says to run one session in one room. The host cues the teacher and the kids. The teacher is the person playing the teacher.

The script covers the talk before Phase A, the change into each of the ten beats, each teacher-state change, and both finales. It has 21 cues. A host can read a cue and look back up.

The ten beats stay in order. This script adds no beat, prop, timer, score, send-back, or joke.

## 2. The design, concrete enough that a host could run it

Say the quoted line. Read one branch, the one that matches the locked choice or the teacher’s current activity. Skip a cue whose *If* is false. Lines in italics are for the host.

Tags sit under the line so a decision is not mistaken for a source requirement. Say a RECOMMENDATION line only when the group is using section 4.

The only steps back in the session are cue 11. The three Phase A calls are Start to wake, Wake, and Eyes open. They stay in Phase A.

### C01 — Before Phase A: the session

**Say:** "One session. Phase A, then Phase B. Beats one through ten, in that order. We win the session after the confetti blast and the silly string finale. Beat 10 stays after Phase A."

DESIGN-DECISION 4.

### C02 — Before Phase A: places

**Say:** "Teacher, sleep at your desk at the front. You are guarding the prize. Kids, you start at the back. You sneak up to grab the prize."

### C03 — Before Phase A: the six choices

*Say one option in each blank. The lists are the real choices. Prize: a golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats. Under the feet: a squeaky dog toy, or bubble wrap. Blast: pop a safe party popper, or throw a handful of paper confetti. Strict activity: facing the whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book. Shadow: a hat, or giant ears. Spray: the teacher’s desk, or the teacher’s shoes.*

**Say:** "Prize, [one]. Under your feet, [one]. Blast, [one]. Strict activity, [one]. On the shadow, [one]. Silly string hits [one]."

### C04 — Before Phase A: the three calls

**Say:** "Three calls. Start to wake opens the plush window. Wake freezes every kid in a ridiculous pose. Eyes open makes anyone still moving a statue. During the five-second laugh, you may move. Outside that laugh, a wake freezes you."

DESIGN-DECISION 1. DESIGN-DECISION 2.

### C05 — Beat 1, The Squeaky Toy Shoes

**Say:** "Beat 1, Squeaky Toy Shoes. Walk across the room with [the squeaky dog toy / the bubble wrap] under your feet. Silent sneaking is impossible. The prize is still ahead."

### C06 — Beat 2, The Feather Tickle Trigger

*The beat title names no feather prop. The thrown object is the soft plush toy.*

**Say:** "Beat 2, Feather Tickle Trigger. Teacher, keep sleeping. The plush throw waits for start to wake."

### C07 — Start to wake

*If the host is opening the feather window. Skip this cue when that window stays closed, and go on to C10 when the group leaves beat 2.*

**Say:** "Start to wake. That is the plush window. A kid may throw the soft plush onto the shield mat."

DESIGN-DECISION 1.

### C08 — The five-second laugh

*If the soft plush lands on the shield mat.*

**Say:** "The plush is on the mat. Teacher, laugh for five seconds. Kids, you may move. Five. Four. Three. Two. One. The laugh is over. The move window is closed."

DESIGN-DECISION 2. *Hold Wake and Eyes open until this count is finished.*

**RECOMMENDATION, say:** "Teacher, sleep again. You are guarding the prize."

### C09 — Wake

*If the teacher wakes during Phase A and the five-second laugh is not running. This call is not a beat. It belongs to Phase A.*

**Say:** "Wake. Every kid, freeze in a ridiculous pose."

DESIGN-DECISION 1. DESIGN-DECISION 2.

### C10 — Beat 3, The Copycat Pose

**Say:** "Beat 3, Copycat Pose. Teacher, open your eyes. Anyone still moving, freeze and be a statue. One example is balancing on one foot with a finger on the nose."

DESIGN-DECISION 1.

### C11 — Three steps back

*If a kid who was caught moving laughs.*

**Say:** "You were caught moving, and you laughed. Three steps back."

**RECOMMENDATION, say:** "Back is toward the back of the room."

### C12 — Beat 4, The Sticky Note Sabotage

**Say:** "Beat 4, Sticky Note Sabotage. To pass the checkpoint line, sneak a sticky note onto the teacher’s back. A mustache, or a goofy face, is the example. Teacher, let the note stay unnoticed. Then pass the line."

### C13 — Beat 5, the confetti finale

*Popper branch:*

**Say:** "Beat 5, Confetti Victory Blast. You reached the desk without getting caught. Pop the safe party popper and claim the prize. Phase A ends. Phase B is next. The session waits for the silly string finale."

*Confetti branch:*

**Say:** "Beat 5, Confetti Victory Blast. You reached the desk without getting caught. Throw a handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head and claim the prize. Phase A ends. Phase B is next. The session waits for the silly string finale."

DESIGN-DECISION 4.

### C14 — Phase B starts

**Say:** "Phase B starts now, in this room. Teacher, stand up. You are the strict teacher. Pretend to be very strict."

*Then one activity line.*

**Say:** "Face the whiteboard."

**Say:** "Type on the computer."

**Say:** "Read the big book."

**RECOMMENDATION, say:** "Kids, return to the back of the room."

**Say:** "The props stay in the room. Deliver the secret note to the mailbox on the other side of the room."

*The three Phase A calls stay in Phase A.*

### C15 — Beat 6, The Whoopee Cushion Pathway

**Say:** "Beat 6, Whoopee Cushion Pathway. The floor is lined with cushions. Three of them hide a whoopee cushion. Guess which cushions are safe. A wrong step makes a loud noise."

### C16 — Beat 7, The Reverse Homework Trick

**Say:** "Beat 7, Reverse Homework Trick. Instead of work, pass the giant clown sunglasses down the line. Use only your feet. Keep hold of them."

### C17 — Beat 8, The Doodle Attack

*Read the branch that matches what the teacher is doing now.*

*Already facing the whiteboard:*

**Say:** "Beat 8, Doodle Attack. Teacher, turn away from the whiteboard, then turn back to face it. Kids, that turn back is your window. Race to the teacher’s shadow on the wall. Draw with the flashlight: a hat, or giant ears."

*Typing or reading:*

**Say:** "Beat 8, Doodle Attack. Teacher, turn to face the whiteboard. Kids, that turn is your window. Race to the teacher’s shadow on the wall. Draw with the flashlight: a hat, or giant ears."

DESIGN-DECISION 3.

### C18 — Beat 9, The Monster Talk Distraction

**Say:** "Beat 9, Monster Talk Distraction. One kid, yell a phrase in a deep monster voice. The example is: The broccoli has landed! Teacher, turn around, confused, and investigate the sound. Other kids, you can crawl forward."

### C19 — Beat 10 opens

**Say:** "Beat 10, Silly String Finale. The finale starts once the secret note reaches the mailbox."

### C20 — The mailbox, Phase B won

*When the secret note reaches the mailbox. Read C21 in the same breath.*

**Say:** "The secret note is in the mailbox. Phase B is won. The session waits until the spray joins the confetti blast."

DESIGN-DECISION 4.

### C21 — The silly string finale, session won

*Desk branch:*

**Say:** "Gently spray the teacher’s desk with silly string. Victory dance while you spray. The confetti blast and the silly string finale have both happened. The session is won."

*Shoes branch:*

**Say:** "Gently spray the teacher’s shoes with silly string. Victory dance while you spray. The confetti blast and the silly string finale have both happened. The session is won."

DESIGN-DECISION 4.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

**DESIGN-DECISION 1.** Three wake moments. "Starts to wake" is only the feather-trigger window. "Wakes up" freezes every kid in a ridiculous pose. "Opens their eyes" is the copycat statue for anyone still moving.

Used in C04, C07, C09, and C10.

**DESIGN-DECISION 2.** During the 5-second laugh, kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them.

Used in C04, C08, and C09.

**DESIGN-DECISION 3.** "The board" means the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the doodle window opens when they turn away and then turn back. If they are typing or reading, the doodle window is when they turn to face the whiteboard.

Used in C17.

**DESIGN-DECISION 4.** Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales. "Win the game" in beat 10 does not skip Phase A.

Used in C01, C13, C20, and C21. The spoken line says Phase B is won at the mailbox, and the session is won after the spray. The host does not say "win the game."

## 4. Recommendations for UNKNOWN facts

These are the smallest rules that let one group play. They are not requirements.

1. If a real choice is still open when the host reaches C03, the host picks one listed option and keeps that fill for the spoken branch. Who would have picked it, and when a pick locks, stays UNKNOWN.
2. If the host never uses C07, the host goes on to beat 3. The plush stays unthrown. No wake is added to produce the throw.
3. After the five-count in C08, the teacher returns to sleeping and guarding the prize, so the rest of Phase A can play on a sleeping teacher.
4. At C14, the kids walk to the back of the room before beat 6. That walk is the phase join. It is not a beat.
5. In C11, "back" faces the back of the room, where the kids started Phase A.
6. The host speaks the next beat cue after the kids have done that beat’s written action: the walk, the checkpoint stick, a cushion step or its noise, a feet-only pass that keeps hold of the sunglasses, or one monster yell with the teacher’s turn. The host’s call is only the cue order. It is not a finish test, a score, or a failure.
7. On beat 4 the teacher plays the note as unnoticed, so a kid can pass the line on the written success.

None.

## 6. Open questions you refused to guess

1. How many kids play, and whether every kid must finish beats 1–4 before one kid’s claim ends Phase A.
2. How far the beat 1 walk goes, and whether beats 1–5 are separate gates or one crossing. The cues stay in order either way.
3. What causes start-to-wake, wake, or eyes-open, apart from the host speaking that call. No noise is written as a cause.
4. How long a ridiculous pose or a statue is held, and what a kid who was already still does when the eyes open. A kid already holding a wake pose is not given a new pose here.
5. Whether eyes-open can overlap the five-second laugh in the rules. The host keeps those calls outside the count.
6. What the teacher does at the end of the laugh, beyond the recommendation to sleep and guard again. Whether "everyone" who may move includes the teacher.
7. What "caught" means on the prize claim, and whether it is the same as "caught moving."
8. Whether a kid who was not caught moving, and who laughs, takes three steps back. How long a step is, and what that kid does after the three steps.
9. Whether one unnoticed note lets every kid pass the line. What the teacher does on noticing the note. The beat writes no send-back, and this script adds none.
10. Whether a statue, a sticky-note picture, or a monster phrase other than the named examples counts.
11. What ends beats 6, 7, and 9 in the source. Where the kids go after a whoopee noise. Where a dropped pair of sunglasses returns. What happens if the pass uses something other than feet.
12. What to do if the plush misses the mat, the teacher notices the note, the sunglasses drop, or the teacher never makes the beat 8 turn. No failure result and no replay is written.
13. Whether the strict-teacher activity changes before beat 8. C17 follows whatever the teacher is doing at the doodle.
14. What the secret note says, who carries it, and which action moves it. Whether the yelling kid may crawl. Which movements are the victory dance, who sprays, and how much silly string a gentle spray uses.
15. Where the shield mat, the checkpoint line, the mailbox, the shadow, and the prize sit during Phase B. What the carried Phase A props do in beats 6–10.
