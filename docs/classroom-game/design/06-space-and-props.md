# Space and props

## 1. What this slice designs

This slice places the one classroom and all 22 props named in the core document (section 4). For each prop it says where it sits, who touches it, and when it is in play. It also says what is still in the room at the instant Phase B starts.

A picture on the sticky note, and a hat or giant ears on the shadow, stay drawings. They are not props. The beat title The Squeaky Toy Shoes is the under-foot walk. The teacher’s shoes are the shoes the teacher is wearing. No feather is placed. Beats 3 and 9 add no prop. Beat 3 is a pose. Beat 9 is a yell.

Places the requirements already fix:

- One room for the whole session. Phase B starts in that room.
- The teacher’s desk is at the front. Phase A starts with the teacher sleeping there, guarding the target prize, and the kids at the back.
- The kids sneak from the back toward that prize. Beat 1 is a walk across the room. The mid-way checkpoint line is the beat 4 gate. Reaching the desk is beat 5.
- The target mailbox is on the other side of the room, and it is a mailbox of its own.
- In beat 6 the floor is lined with cushions, and three of those cushions have hidden whoopee cushions underneath.
- In beat 8 the teacher’s shadow is on a wall, and the kids draw on it with a flashlight when the teacher turns to face the board.
- Props from Phase A are still in the room for Phase B. The desk beat 10 may spray is this same desk.

Everything below that fills an open place is a recommendation in section 4. Section 2 uses those recommendations so a host can set the room and run it.

## 2. The design

### The room

The room has two ends. The **back** is the wall the kids start against. The **front** is the wall the desk sits against. Forward is from the back toward the front. Back, for the three steps in beat 3, is toward that back wall. This design sets no length for a step and no size for the room.

Two floor paths run from the back to the front. They sit side by side and do not cover each other.

- The **open path** is clear floor from the back wall to the front edge of the desk. Phase A walking uses this path. The checkpoint line crosses this path only.
- The **cushion line** is one file of cushions along the other side of the floor, from the back wall to the mailbox. Beat 6 uses this line. The whoopee cushions are already hidden under three of these cushions before the kids enter.

The host picks which physical side of the room is the cushion-line side. The other side is the shield side.

At the front, in this order from the shield side to the cushion-line side:

1. The shield mat, on the floor beside the desk, off both paths.
2. The teacher’s desk, against the front wall.
3. The whiteboard, on the front wall beside the desk. This is the board (DESIGN-DECISION 3).
4. The mailbox, in the front corner on the cushion-line side, at a height the kids can reach, off the desk. The cushion line ends at the mailbox.

The other side of the room, for the mailbox, is the front: the end opposite the back where the kids start.

On the desktop, from the start: the one locked target prize nearest the class, the computer on one side, and the big book shut on the other side. The teacher sleeps at the desk over the prize. The teacher’s shoes are on the teacher’s feet for the whole session.

A holding cluster sits on the floor at the back, off the open path, on the shield side. Waiting there before play: the soft plush, the sticky note, the locked under-foot items, the locked blast object, the secret note face down, the clown sunglasses, the flashlight, and the silly string.

### How a host runs the room

Before the kids enter, the host places every prop, hides the three whoopee cushions, marks the checkpoint line, and locks the real choices in section 4. The host tells the kids which mat is the shield mat, which object is the target prize, and that the cushion line waits until Phase B.

**Phase A open.** The teacher is at the desk, sleeping, shoes on, guarding the prize on the desktop. The kids stand at the back on the open path. Phase A hand props (plush, sticky note, under-foot items, blast object) are with the kids. The Phase B hand props stay in the holding cluster until Phase B.

**Beat 1.** Each walking kid puts the locked under-foot item between a foot and the open-path floor and walks from the back wall to the checkpoint tape. They stop at the tape. They may use a hand to keep the item under the foot. After the walk, those items go back to the holding cluster and stay there.

**Beat 2.** The plush is in play only in the “starts to wake” window (DESIGN-DECISION 1). A kid throws it onto the shield mat. If it lands on the mat, it stays on the mat for the rest of the session, and the teacher laughs for 5 seconds. During those 5 seconds the kids may move along the open path, including past the tape (DESIGN-DECISION 2). The plush stays on the mat while they move. Outside that laugh, a wake freezes the kids where they stand. A frozen kid keeps any prop already in their hands. If the throw misses, the same kid picks the plush up, and a kid may throw it again the next time the teacher starts to wake. If the teacher never starts to wake, the plush stays with the kids and the mat stays empty. This design adds no wake.

**Beat 3.** No prop. Anyone sent three steps back moves toward the back wall along the open path. Step length is open (section 6).

**Beat 4.** The tape is the gate. A kid carries the sticky note up the open path, crosses the tape by sticking that note on the teacher’s back, and the teacher does not notice. The kid is the one who touches the note. The teacher leaves it where it was stuck. It rides on the teacher’s back for the rest of the session, including after the teacher stands.

**Beat 5.** A kid goes from the tape to the desk along the open path, takes the prize off the desktop, and at the same time does the locked blast. Confetti, if that is the blast, is thrown so it passes over the teacher’s head and then lies where it falls, on and around the desk. A popper, if that is the blast, is popped at the desk and then set on the desk. The claiming kid keeps the prize.

**Phase join.** The teacher stands up at the desk and becomes the strict teacher. The kids walk the open path back to the back wall. They carry the claimed prize, and they pick up the secret note, the flashlight, and the silly string from the holding cluster. This walk is the join between the phases. It is not a beat.

**Phase B open.** The kids are at the back. The teacher is standing and doing the one locked activity. If that activity is the whiteboard, the teacher stands at the front, facing the whiteboard, back toward the open path, and the shadow is on the front wall beside the whiteboard. If the activity is the computer, the teacher stands at the desk and types. If the activity is the book, the teacher stands at the desk and reads the open book. The unused activity objects stay on the desk. The checkpoint tape is still on the floor and does not gate Phase B.

**Beat 6.** The kids step the cushion line from the back end toward the mailbox. They step on the tops and leave the cushions down, so the whoopee cushions stay hidden. A wrong step sounds the whoopee under that cushion. The kid stays on that cushion. The whoopee stays under it. The secret note, flashlight, and silly string stay in hands and are not used as part of the step.

**Beat 7.** The kids stand in a row on the open floor beside the cushion line, at the depth the group has reached. A kid brings the sunglasses from the holding cluster along the open path and sets them on the floor at the host’s start end of the row. During the pass, only feet touch the pair. The pair moves from the start end to the other end. After the pass, the pair stays on the floor at the finish end for the rest of the session.

**Beat 8.** The drawing window follows DESIGN-DECISION 3. If the teacher is already facing the whiteboard, the kids wait while the teacher turns away and draw when the teacher turns back. If the teacher is typing or reading, the kids draw when the teacher turns to face the whiteboard. In that window the shadow is on the front wall beside the whiteboard, cast by the room’s ordinary light. The kids race along the open path to that shadow. The kid who is carrying the flashlight draws the locked picture, a hat or giant ears, with the beam. Nothing is stuck on the wall. The flashlight stays with that kid afterward.

**Beat 9.** No prop. The kid who is holding the secret note may crawl with it still in hand. Forward is toward the mailbox. The note stays in the hand.

**Beat 10.** When this beat is the current beat, the kid who is holding the secret note puts it into the mailbox. It stays in the mailbox. That arrival starts the finale. The kids then gently spray the locked target, the desk or the shoes, with the silly string, and they do the victory dance beside that target (DESIGN-DECISION 4). The string stays where it landed. Beat 10’s Phase B win does not clear the room. The session still needs both finales; the confetti victory blast is the Phase A finale and has already happened if the beats ran in order.

### What stays in the room when Phase B starts

Phase B starts in this same room, at the moment the prize has been claimed with the blast and the teacher stands up. Nothing from Phase A is removed. The Phase B props are already in the room. They were set before play, off the open path.

Still in the room, and where each one is at that instant:

1. The teacher’s desk, at the front, with the teacher standing up from it.
2. The target prize, in the claiming kid’s hands, going back to the back wall with the kids.
3. The locked under-foot items, in the back holding cluster, off everyone’s feet. The unlocked under-foot kind was never brought in.
4. The soft plush, on the shield mat if a throw landed, otherwise still with the kids.
5. The shield mat, unmoved, beside the desk.
6. The checkpoint tape, unmoved, across the open path.
7. The sticky note, on the teacher’s back.
8. The spent blast object: confetti lying on and around the desk, or the spent popper sitting on the desk. The unlocked blast object was never brought in.
9. The whiteboard, on the front wall.
10. The computer, on the desk.
11. The big book, on the desk until the teacher opens it for a reading activity.
12. The secret note, picked up from the holding cluster as the kids reach the back.
13. The mailbox, empty, in the front corner.
14. The cushions, already lining the side path.
15. The three whoopee cushions, still hidden under three of those cushions.
16. The clown sunglasses, in the holding cluster until beat 7.
17. The teacher’s shadow, on the front wall if the teacher is facing the whiteboard, and not yet the play shadow if the teacher is typing or reading.
18. The flashlight, picked up from the holding cluster with the secret note.
19. The silly string, picked up from the holding cluster, unopened.
20. The teacher’s shoes, on the teacher’s feet.

The two choice props that were not locked (one under-foot kind, and either the popper or the confetti) are the only named props absent. Every prop that was in the room for Phase A is still in the room. The kids are at the back. The teacher is standing.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

**DESIGN-DECISION 1.** Three wake moments. “Starts to wake” is the only window in which a kid may throw the plush onto the shield mat. “Wakes up” freezes the kids in a pose where they stand, still holding whatever is in their hands. “Opens their eyes” is the copycat statue for anyone still moving. The mat is not used for those two freezes.

**DESIGN-DECISION 2.** During the 5-second laugh, the kids may move, including along the open path past the checkpoint tape. The plush stays on the mat during that window. Outside the laugh, a wake freezes them among the props where they are.

**DESIGN-DECISION 3.** The board is the whiteboard on the front wall. If the strict teacher is already facing it, the doodle window opens when they turn away and then turn back, and the shadow is on the front wall on that return. If they are typing or reading, the doodle window is the turn to face the whiteboard, and the shadow appears on that wall for the turn.

**DESIGN-DECISION 4.** Beat 10 wins Phase B. Putting the secret note in the mailbox starts the silly-string finale; it does not skip the spray, and it does not clear the room. The session is won only after the confetti victory blast and the silly string finale have both happened. A Phase A claim does not end the session.

## 4. Recommendations for UNKNOWN facts

These are the smallest rules that let one group play. They are not source requirements.

1. **Who places the props (unknown 46).** The host places all of them before the kids enter, hides the whoopee cushions, marks the tape, names the shield mat, and names the prize.
2. **When Phase B props are placed (unknowns 27, 34, 49).** They are already in the room during Phase A, off the open path. The cushion line does not cover the tape, the mat, or the sneak path. No one moves Phase A props aside to make room for beat 6.
3. **Where the mailbox, whiteboard, computer, and book sit (unknown 28).** The front is the other side from the back. The mailbox is in the front corner, off the desk. The whiteboard is on the front wall beside the desk. The computer and the book sit on the desk.
4. **Unused strict-teacher objects (unknown 30).** The computer and the book stay on the desk when they are not the activity. The host locks one activity before play, and the teacher keeps that activity until beat 8 or beat 9 turns them.
5. **Prize kind (unknown 24).** The host locks one kind. Only that object is set out. If the kind is “a toy,” the host shows one object and names it as the prize, and that object is not the plush, the sunglasses, or a whoopee cushion.
6. **Under-foot items (unknown 26).** The host locks one kind for every walking kid, one piece each. The other kind stays out of the room. After beat 1 the pieces return to the holding cluster and stay off the feet.
7. **How far beat 1 goes, and what the line is (unknowns 3, 4, 19).** The line is tape across the open path, placed by eye halfway along the journey from the back wall to the front edge of the desk. No one measures the room. Beat 1 stops at the tape. Beat 4 crosses it. Beat 5 runs from the tape to the desk.
8. **The shield mat (unknown 20).** One mat, host-named, on the shield-side floor beside the desk. One plush. A landing throw stays on the mat and is not thrown again. A miss goes back into a kid’s hands and may be thrown the next time the teacher starts to wake. The laugh happens when the plush is on the mat.
9. **The sticky note (unknowns 17, 18, 21, 33).** One note. The host draws either the mustache or the goofy face before play. One kid carries it and sticks it. It stays on the teacher’s back after the teacher stands. Whether any other picture would count stays open.
10. **The blast objects (unknowns 22, 23).** The host locks one. Only that object is set out: one popper, or one handful, meaning the pieces one kid can hold in one hand. Confetti passes over the teacher’s head and stays where it falls. The popper is popped at the desk, then set on the desk. This design does not send the popper over the head, and it does not set both objects out.
11. **The claimed prize in Phase B (unknown 33).** It stays in the claiming kid’s hands.
12. **The path to the back (unknown 27).** After the claim, the kids walk the open path to the back wall while the teacher stands up.
13. **The teacher’s seat during Phase A (unknowns 15, 31).** The teacher stays at the desk through Phase A, including the 5-second laugh, and stands at the Phase B start.
14. **The cushions (unknown 35).** More than three, in one file. Exactly three of them hide one whoopee each. The host picks which three and leaves them unmarked. A sounded whoopee stays. The kids do not lift the cushions.
15. **After a whoopee noise (unknown 9).** The kid stays on that cushion.
16. **The sunglasses (unknowns 10, 36).** One pair. Before the pass, a kid sets them on the floor at one end of a kid row on the open floor. The host points to that end. Feet take them to the other end. After the pass they stay on the floor there.
17. **The shadow and the flashlight (unknown 38).** Ordinary room light casts the shadow on the front wall beside the whiteboard. One flashlight is the drawing tool. The kids draw with the beam and put nothing on the wall.
18. **The secret note (unknown 40).** It is first picked up at the Phase B open, carried in one hand, and put into the mailbox only when beat 10 is the current beat. It then stays in the mailbox. This design writes no words on it.
19. **The silly string (unknown 41).** One can, carried unused until the note is in the mailbox. The host locks the desk or the shoes before play. The kids spray that one target and dance beside it. The string stays where it landed.
20. **Phase A movement rules after the stand-up (unknown 50).** The tape and the sticky-note gate apply to beat 4. They do not gate Phase B. The tape stays.
21. **A freeze while holding a prop.** The kid keeps hold of what is already in their hands.
22. **Shared props (unknown 2).** A beat that says “a kid” uses one shared object, so any kid can be the one who throws, sticks, claims, carries, or sprays. This design does not decide whether the other kids must finish beats 1–4 first.

## 5. Requirement fixes

None. The 22 names in section 4 of the core document are the 22 props placed here. The core document already keeps the secret note as a note, keeps one desk, and leaves the whiteboard and the board as DB-3. DESIGN-DECISION 3 resolves that for play. It is not a change to the requirement text.
