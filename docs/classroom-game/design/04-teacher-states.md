# Teacher states

A person can play the teacher as ten states. Nine are the performances this slice was asked for. **Wakes up** is the tenth, because DESIGN-DECISION 1 gives it its own body: the pose freeze for every kid. Eyes open stays the statue check. The host calls the states. Kid noise never forces one.

The only send-back in the session is the three steps in Eyes open, and only for a kid who was caught moving and then laughs. Every other result below is a permission, a required freeze the source already writes, or a recommendation labeled as such.

## 1. What this slice designs

What the teacher’s body is doing, and what the kids are allowed to do while it is doing that.

In:

- Asleep, starting to wake, laughing for 5 seconds, wakes up, eyes open.
- Standing up into Phase B, then one strict activity: facing the whiteboard, typing, or reading.
- The doodle turn, performed from whichever activity is in force.
- Turned away investigating after the monster-voice yell.
- The silly string finale as kid permission on the activity the teacher is already in. It does not add a teacher state.

Out:

- Where the mat, the line, the mailbox, the shadow, the computer, and the book sit.
- A new punishment, a new timer, a score, a second board, a second desk, or a replay rule.
- New jokes, new poses, and new beats.

Beat order stays 1 through 10. Phase A is beats 1–5. Phase B is beats 6–10 in the same room, with the same props still there.

## 2. The design

### How the host calls a state

The host says the state out loud and performs it. “Immediately” and “instantly” mean the kids’ answer is the next thing they do. The host does not count those words. The only count in the session is the 5-second laugh.

A wake freeze is in force only outside that laugh. During the laugh, the kids may move.

### Asleep

The teacher sits at the teacher’s desk at the front and sleeps. The target prize is on the teacher’s watch: a golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats. The host picks one before the session. The kids start at the back.

While the teacher is asleep, the kids are allowed to:

- Sneak from the back toward the prize.
- On beat 1, walk across the room with a squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap under their feet. Silent sneaking is impossible on that walk. The sound does not change the teacher’s state. The host does not answer the squeak by waking.
- On beat 4, one kid may sneak to the teacher and stick a sticky note on the teacher’s back. A drawing of a mustache and a drawing of a goofy face are the samples that count. If the host does not notice the note, that kid has met the pass: sneak, note, unnoticed. The group may cross the mid-way checkpoint line with that kid. See Recommendations for the one-note reading.
- On beat 5, a kid who reaches the desk without getting caught may claim the prize. The claim’s blast is one of the two written actions: pop a safe party popper, or throw a handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head. Either action is the confetti victory blast. The claim with that blast ends Phase A.

The host leaves the teacher Asleep through the note and the approach. Noticing the note is a spoken “noticed,” and that notice withholds the pass. It does not add a freeze and it does not add steps. What the group does after a noticed note is an open question.

### Starting to wake

DESIGN-DECISION 1. This state is only the feather-trigger window for beat 2.

The teacher stirs at the desk. The kids are allowed to keep moving as they could while Asleep. One kid may throw a soft plush toy onto the designated shield mat. The host designates that mat before Phase A. The throw is allowed. It is not required.

If the plush lands on the mat, the teacher goes to Laughing for 5 seconds. If it misses, or nobody throws, the teacher does not laugh. The host returns to Asleep. Nobody freezes. The host does not call Wakes up in order to make the throw happen.

### Laughing for 5 seconds

The plush on the mat puts the teacher into uncontrollable laughter. The host counts 5 seconds. That is the only timer.

DESIGN-DECISION 2. For those 5 seconds the kids are allowed to move. The window is the exception. The host does not call Wakes up during it, and does not call Eyes open during it. Outside it, a wake freezes them.

When the count ends, the teacher stops laughing and the host returns to Asleep. The next beat is still ahead of them. The laugh does not claim the prize and does not stand the teacher up.

### Wakes up

DESIGN-DECISION 1. This is the opening freeze, and it is a different performance from Starting to wake and from Eyes open.

The teacher wakes at the desk in a way the whole room can see. Every kid is allowed one response: freeze at once in a ridiculous pose. The source gives that response as one action. The host does not hand out a pose menu, and the host does not grade the pose. “Ridiculous” and “funny” stay tone.

The kids hold the pose until the host returns the teacher to Asleep. A laugh during this pose does not send anyone back. The three steps belong only to Eyes open.

The host may call Wakes up whenever the current state is Asleep, and not during the 5-second laugh. A squeak, a whoopee, a dropped pass, or a sticky note does not force this call.

### Eyes open

DESIGN-DECISION 1. Beat 3. The teacher opens their eyes suddenly. This is the copycat statue for anyone still moving.

At that instant:

- A kid who is moving freezes and imitates a statue. Balancing on one foot with a finger on the nose is the sample that counts. The host treats “still moving” as “caught moving” for this state. See Recommendations.
- A kid who was still is allowed to keep doing what the current beat allows. That kid is not put in the statue and is not given the three steps.
- If a kid who was caught moving laughs, that kid takes three steps back, toward the back of the room, in that kid’s own steps. Then that kid rejoins the session with no further send-back. This is the only send-back the session writes.

The host performs Eyes open once, as beat 3, with the teacher outside the laugh. After the statues have been seen, the host returns the teacher to Asleep so beat 4 can use the sleeping back for the sticky note.

### Standing into Phase B

The host uses this state at the moment Phase A ends: a kid has claimed the prize with the confetti victory blast. The teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher, pretending to be very strict. The room and the Phase A props stay.

The kids are allowed to take the Phase B opening at the back of the room. The kid who was at the desk walks back with the group. That walk has no freeze and no steps. Phase A’s pose, statue, and three steps are not in force after the stand.

DESIGN-DECISION 4. Standing up does not win the session. Beat 10 has not happened. “Win the game” on the mailbox cannot be played here, and it cannot skip the blast that just happened.

Before beat 6, the host ends this state by entering one activity: Facing the whiteboard, Typing, or Reading. Any one of the three is enough. The host picks one and says it.

### Facing the whiteboard, Typing, and Reading

These three are the strict-teacher activities. The teacher pretends to be very strict in all of them.

- Facing the whiteboard: the teacher stands facing the whiteboard.
- Typing: the teacher types on a computer.
- Reading: the teacher reads a big book.

While the teacher is in the chosen activity, the kids are allowed to:

- Carry the secret note toward the target mailbox on the other side of the room.
- On beat 6, guess which cushions are safe to step on. The floor is lined with cushions. Three of them have a whoopee cushion underneath. A wrong step makes a loud noise. The teacher stays in the activity. The noise is the result the beat writes. It does not send the kids back and it does not wake anyone.
- On beat 7, pass a giant oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line, using only their feet, without dropping them. The teacher stays in the activity.

The teacher has no written reaction to the whoopee or to the sunglasses. The host does not invent one.

#### The doodle turn

DESIGN-DECISION 3. “The board” is the whiteboard. There is one board.

Beat 8 opens the doodle window by a turn, and the turn depends on the activity already in force:

- The teacher is already Facing the whiteboard. The host turns them away from it, then turns them back to face it. The window opens on the turn back.
- The teacher is Typing or Reading. The host turns them to face the whiteboard. The window opens on that turn.

When the window opens, the kids are allowed to race to the teacher’s shadow on the wall and draw on it with a flashlight. The drawing is a hat, or giant ears. “Quickly” is not a count. A missed drawing has no written punishment, so the host adds none. The beat can be left once the window has opened and the kids have had the race, whether or not a drawing landed.

After the window, the teacher is Facing the whiteboard. That is a recommendation, so the body has one end pose. See Recommendations.

### Turned away investigating

Beat 9. One kid yells a phrase in a deep monster voice. “The broccoli has landed!” is the sample that counts. On that yell, the teacher turns away from the current activity and investigates the sound. The manner is confused. That word is tone. The host does not grade the face.

While the teacher is turned away, the other kids are allowed to crawl forward. The yelling kid’s written action is the yell. The crawl permission is written for the other kids. The host does not freeze anyone, and does not send anyone back, for the yell or for the crawl.

The host ends the state by turning the teacher back to the activity in force when the yell happened. After a doodle turn, that activity is Facing the whiteboard if the recommendation above was used. No second count is added. How long the investigation lasts is the host’s hold, long enough that the other kids were allowed to crawl.

### Silly string, with no new state

Beat 10 starts once the secret note reaches the target mailbox. The teacher stays in the current strict activity, or has just returned to it from the investigation. The kids are allowed to gently spray silly string on the teacher’s desk or on the teacher’s shoes, and to do a victory dance while they spray. The desk they may spray is the teacher’s desk from Phase A.

DESIGN-DECISION 4. That finale wins Phase B. The session is won only when this finale and the confetti victory blast have both happened. The blast is already required before the teacher can stand, so a group that reaches the mailbox in order has both. “Win the game” in beat 10 does not skip Phase A, and it does not replace the session win.

The teacher does not get a separate win. The host does not add a flinch, a chase, or a scolding state for the string.

### One way to run the session

1. Host picks the prize, designates the shield mat, and seats the teacher Asleep at the front. Kids at the back.
2. Beat 1, still Asleep. Kids walk with a squeaky toy or bubble wrap under their feet.
3. The host may call Wakes up once during an Asleep stretch outside the laugh. Every kid poses. The host returns to Asleep before the next beat action. Skip this call if the host wants the “if” to stay unused. The rule is still in the session.
4. Beat 2. Host calls Starting to wake. A kid may throw the plush. On a hit, Laughing for 5 seconds, kids move, then Asleep. On a miss or no throw, Asleep, and nobody is frozen.
5. Beat 3. Host calls Eyes open. Moving kids statue. A caught kid who laughs takes three steps back. Then Asleep.
6. Beat 4, Asleep. One kid sticks a note the host does not notice. The group crosses the checkpoint line.
7. Beat 5, Asleep. A kid who was not caught reaches the desk, claims the prize, and pops the popper or throws the confetti over the teacher’s head.
8. Standing into Phase B. Kids go to the back. Host picks Facing the whiteboard, Typing, or Reading.
9. Beats 6 and 7 in that activity. Whoopee noise and the sunglasses pass leave the teacher where they are.
10. Beat 8. Doodle turn, as the activity requires. Kids race and draw. Teacher ends Facing the whiteboard.
11. Beat 9. One kid yells. Teacher goes to Turned away investigating. The other kids may crawl. Host turns the teacher back.
12. Beat 10. The note is in the mailbox. Kids spray the desk or the shoes and dance. Phase B is won. The session is won because the blast already happened.

## 3. DESIGN-DECISION lines used

**DESIGN-DECISION 1.** Three wake moments. Starting to wake is only the feather-trigger window. Wakes up freezes every kid in a ridiculous pose. Eyes open is the copycat statue for anyone still moving. Used in Starting to wake, Wakes up, and Eyes open. The host never stacks the throw, the pose, and the statue into one stir.

**DESIGN-DECISION 2.** During the 5-second laugh, kids may move. That window is the exception. Outside it, a wake freezes them. Used in Laughing for 5 seconds. The host also holds Eyes open until the laugh is over, so the statue is not called in the same window that allows movement.

**DESIGN-DECISION 3.** The board is the whiteboard. If the teacher is already facing it, the doodle window opens when they turn away and then turn back. If they are typing or reading, the doodle window is when they turn to face the whiteboard. Used in the doodle turn. No second board is added.

**DESIGN-DECISION 4.** Beat 10 wins Phase B. The session is won only after both finales. “Win the game” in beat 10 does not skip Phase A. Used in Standing into Phase B and in the silly string finale.

## 4. Recommendations for UNKNOWN facts

These are the smallest host rules that let one group play. They are not requirements.

1. **What starts a wake (unknown 16).** The host performs Starting to wake for beat 2 and Eyes open for beat 3, because those beats are in the order. Kid noise does not cause them. Wakes up stays the host’s “if.”
2. **Leaving beat 2 (unknown 5).** If the teacher never receives a plush on the mat, the host returns to Asleep and continues. No wake is added to force the throw.
3. **When the laugh ends (unknown 15).** The teacher returns to Asleep. The laugh does not open the eyes and does not stand the teacher up.
4. **“Everyone” during the laugh (unknown 15).** The kids move. The teacher uses the 5 seconds to laugh. The source word “everyone” stays as written in the requirement. This recommendation only keeps the teacher’s body on the laugh.
5. **The three moments do not overlap (unknowns 13 and 15).** The host never has two of Starting to wake, Laughing, Wakes up, and Eyes open in force together. Laughing is the only one with a count, and it occupies its own 5 seconds.
6. **How long a freeze lasts (unknown 14).** Wakes up holds until the host says Asleep. Eyes open holds the statues until the host says Asleep. No extra count.
7. **“Caught” at Eyes open (unknowns 14 and 17).** For this state, caught means the kid was moving when the eyes opened. A kid who was still is left on the current beat’s permissions.
8. **“Caught” on the desk claim (unknown 17).** A kid may claim the prize if they were still at Eyes open, and if they held the Wakes up pose until the host said Asleep on any wake the host did call. A kid who was moving at Eyes open, or who moved during a Wakes up pose, has been caught for that claim. This is the smallest test that gives beat 5 a yes or no. It is not a new penalty on top of the pose or the statue.
9. **Who takes the three steps (unknown 11).** Only a kid who was caught moving and then laughs. Another kid’s laugh does not send them back.
10. **The three steps (unknown 12).** Back is toward the back of the room, where Phase A started. The steps are that kid’s own steps. After the third step, the kid continues with the group.
11. **A missed plush (unknown 20).** The host designates the mat before play. A miss leaves the teacher able to return to Asleep. The plush may be picked up so the room is walkable. Throwing it again is not required. Where the mat sits is still open.
12. **One unnoticed note (unknown 18).** The kid who stuck the unnoticed note has passed the line’s test. The group crosses with that kid. The host does not demand a note from every kid. Player count is unknown, and the source asks for a kid.
13. **A noticed note (unknown 18, and the missing failure rule).** The pass is withheld. The teacher can stay Asleep. No steps and no pose are added. What the group does next is left open.
14. **The walk to the back (unknown 27).** After the blast, the kids walk from the desk to the back. Then the host starts the chosen activity and beat 6. The walk is the join between the claim and the Phase B opening position. It is not a beat.
15. **One activity until a turn requires another (unknown 30).** The host picks one of the three at the stand and keeps it through beats 6 and 7. The computer and the book may sit in the room. Only the chosen activity has to be performed.
16. **After the doodle (unknowns 30 and 37).** The teacher ends Facing the whiteboard, including when they began the phase typing or reading. A missed or wrong drawing changes nothing else. Whether a hat and giant ears together count stays open. The host accepts either one.
17. **Ending the investigation (unknown 39).** The host turns the teacher back to the activity in force at the yell. The other kids were allowed to crawl once. No distance is set for “forward.”
18. **The sticky note in Phase B (unknown 33).** It stays where beat 4 put it. A Phase B turn does not reopen the notice test.
19. **Leaving beats 6, 7, and 9 (unknown 7).** Beat 6 is left when the group has stepped a path across the cushions, noises included. Beat 7 is left when the sunglasses have gone down the line on feet without a drop. Beat 9 is left when the yell has happened and the other kids have been allowed to crawl. None of these exits adds a failure.
20. **The teacher during the finales (unknown 32).** During the blast, the teacher is Asleep until the claim completes, then stands. During the silly string, the teacher stays in the strict activity. No extra reaction is performed.

## 5. Requirement fixes

A later pass can apply these. This slice does not edit the core document.

1. **The names table already splits the three wake phrases, and DB-1 plus unknown 13 say the split is not decided.** Section 2 says wakes up is the opening freeze only, starts to wake up is the plush throw only, and opens their eyes is the statue only. Section 7 says the brief does not say these are one moment or three, and that the document does not pick a side. Unknown 13 repeats the open question. DESIGN-DECISION 1 matches the names table. A later pass should close DB-1 and unknown 13 in favor of that split, or drop the split from the names table. Leaving both will make the next designer restack the three performances.
2. **Section 10 says no Phase B win is added. DESIGN-DECISION 4 says beat 10 wins Phase B.** REQ-MEGA-036 stays the session win: both finales. The retired REQ-MEGA-037 extra label should stay retired. A later pass should record a Phase B win as this decision, in words that cannot be read as a second session win, and should point section 10 and unknown 53 at that decision.
3. **“The board” and the whiteboard are still unjoined in the core.** The names table, section 4, DB-3, and unknown 29 leave them unequated. DESIGN-DECISION 3 says the board is the whiteboard and adds no second prop. A later pass should say that in the names table and in the prop list, and close DB-3 and unknown 29.
4. **REQ-MEGA-042 can be misread as “never perform beat 2.”** The requirement forbids adding a wake to force the plush throw. Starting to wake is beat 2’s own condition. A later pass should add one sentence: performing Starting to wake for beat 2 is the beat, and calling Wakes up to force the throw is the added wake the requirement forbids.

## 6. Open questions this design refuses to guess

- How many kids play, and whether every kid must finish beats 1–4 before one kid’s claim ends Phase A.
- Whether a statue, a sticky-note picture, or a monster phrase other than the named samples counts.
- Whether one drawing may show both a hat and giant ears.
- Whether the yelling kid may also crawl, and whether beat 9 is failed when nobody crawls. This design allows the other kids to crawl and adds no failure either way.
- What the group does after a noticed sticky note. The pass is withheld. No next action is invented.
- Where a dropped pair of sunglasses returns, and what happens if the pass uses something other than feet.
- Where the shield mat, the checkpoint line, the mailbox, the shadow, the computer, and the book are placed.
- What the secret note says, which movements are the victory dance, and how much string a gentle spray is.
- What happens when the spray is not gentle, or when it hits something other than the desk or the shoes.
- Any counted length for a freeze, a doodle window, or an investigation, past the host holds above. No number is recommended.
- Whether the teacher has a reaction to the popper, the confetti, a whoopee, or the silly string beyond the states written here.
- Replay of a beat, a phase, or the session.
