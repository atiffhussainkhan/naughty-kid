# Slice 08 — Options and replay

Requirement level only. Source: Game 1, Game 2, and the merge rule in the classroom-game brief. A real choice is a closed list joined by “or” with no “like”. An example is a sample introduced by “like”. Replay stays UNKNOWN where the text never describes playing a beat, a phase, or the session again.

## 1. Scope

This slice classifies nine named items: prize type, shoe noisemaker, wake response, sticky-note picture, victory effect, what the strict teacher is doing, silly-string target, example statue, and example monster phrase.

| Item | Class | Allowed by the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prize type | Real choice | Golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats |
| Shoe noisemaker | Real choice | Squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap |
| Wake response | Required response | Immediate freeze in a ridiculous, funny pose |
| Sticky-note picture | Only an example | Funny sticky note; mustache and goofy face are samples |
| Victory effect | Real choice | Safe party popper, or a handful of paper confetti |
| What the strict teacher is doing | Real choice | Facing a whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book |
| Silly-string target | Real choice | Teacher’s desk or teacher’s shoes |
| Example statue | Only an example | Goofy statue; one-foot, finger-on-nose is a sample |
| Example monster phrase | Only an example | Silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice; broccoli line is a sample |

In scope: which of those are real choices, which named samples are only examples, and whether the text describes a replay or a reset of a choice.

Out of scope: how a beat is performed apart from that classification, where props sit, how far a kid moves, what “safe” and “gently” mean in practice, and any retry the text does not write.

## 2. Requirements

**REQ-08-001.** The prize the sleeping teacher guards, and that a kid claims at the desk, is one of three kinds: a golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats. Those three are a real choice. (Game 1)

**REQ-08-002.** On the squeaky-shoe crossing, the noisemaker under a kid’s feet is a squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap. Those two are a real choice. The beat title “The Squeaky Toy Shoes” leaves bubble wrap in that choice. (Game 1 beat 1)

**REQ-08-003.** When the teacher wakes, the kids immediately freeze in a ridiculous, funny pose. That wake response is required. It is a real action in the text, and it is not a list of responses to pick from, and it is not a sample pose. (Game 1)

**REQ-08-004.** The feather-tickle throw is its own action: if the teacher starts to wake, a kid can throw a soft plush toy onto the designated shield mat so the teacher laughs for 5 seconds. That throw is not a second wake response in a choice with the freeze. (Game 1 beat 2)

**REQ-08-005.** When the teacher suddenly opens their eyes, anyone caught moving freezes and imitates a goofy statue. Balancing on one foot with a finger on the nose is only an example of that statue. A different goofy statue still meets the pose rule. The text offers no menu of statues. (Game 1 beat 3)

**REQ-08-006.** The mid-room checkpoint uses a funny sticky note on the teacher’s back. A drawing of a mustache and a drawing of a goofy face are only examples of that funny note. They are not a two-picture choice, so a different funny picture still meets the picture rule. (Game 1 beat 4)

**REQ-08-007.** As the kid claims the prize, the victory effect is popping a safe party popper or throwing a handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head. Those two are a real choice. The beat title “The Confetti Victory Blast”, including that name in the merge rule, leaves the safe party popper in that choice. (Game 1 beat 5; Merge rule)

**REQ-08-008.** In the strict-teacher phase, what the teacher is doing is a real choice of three: standing facing a whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book. In every one of those, the teacher is pretending to be very strict. (Game 2)

**REQ-08-009.** The yelled line that lets the other kids crawl is a silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice. “The broccoli has landed!” is only an example of that phrase. It is not required, and the text offers no menu of phrases, so a different silly phrase in that voice still meets the phrase rule. (Game 2 beat 9)

**REQ-08-010.** When the secret note reaches the mailbox, the kids gently spray silly string on the teacher’s desk or on the teacher’s shoes, while doing a victory dance. Desk and shoes are a real choice of target. The victory dance goes with the spray; it is not a third target. (Game 2 beat 10)

## 3. Stages

### Options that exist

Phase A, Game 1:

- Beat setup / prize at the desk: prize type, real choice of three (REQ-08-001). The choice is in force when a kid claims the prize on beat 5.
- Beat 1: shoe noisemaker, real choice of two (REQ-08-002).
- Wake: one required freeze in a ridiculous, funny pose (REQ-08-003). Beat 3 writes a goofy-statue freeze for anyone caught moving; the named pose is only an example (REQ-08-005). Whether those two freezes are the same moment is UNKNOWN.
- Beat 2 plush throw: a written action, not an option in this slice (REQ-08-004).
- Beat 4: funny sticky note; the named pictures are only examples (REQ-08-006).
- Beat 5: victory effect, real choice of two (REQ-08-007).

Phase B, Game 2, same room, starting when the teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher:

- Phase setup: what the strict teacher is doing, real choice of three (REQ-08-008).
- Beat 9: monster phrase; the broccoli line is only an example (REQ-08-009).
- Beat 10: silly-string target, real choice of two (REQ-08-010).

Beats 6, 7, and 8 name no choice in this slice’s nine items.

### What can be set up

The text names the alternatives above. It does not name a picker, a setup step, or a moment when a real choice becomes fixed. Those are UNKNOWN (section 4). Setting an option means the play uses one alternative the text already names.

### What can be reset

The text does not describe replaying a beat, a phase, or the session, and it does not describe clearing a real choice and picking again. Replay and option reset are UNKNOWN.

Game 1 beat 3 does write one setback: a kid who laughs during the statue freeze takes three steps back. That setback is a position penalty inside the beat. It does not replay the beat, the phase, or the session, and it does not reset prize type, shoe noisemaker, victory effect, or any Phase B choice. Other beats write no return-and-retry. The merge rule sends kids back only as a beat’s own rule says; where a beat has no such rule, the return stays UNKNOWN.
