# Slice 01 — Combined session loop

Player-facing requirements for one classroom session made of Game 1 and Game 2. Source: those two games and the merge rule. No new jokes, no scoring, no room the texts do not name.

## 1. Scope

In scope:

- Who is in the session, and what the kids are trying to do.
- Where the kids and the teacher start in each phase.
- What the teacher is doing in Phase A and in Phase B.
- The ten beats, in the written order, kept as the body of the session.
- How Phase A ends, how Phase B begins, and what counts as winning the session.
- What the phase boundary sets up, what a failure is allowed to reset, and which named choices the loop exposes.

Out of scope except as a handoff line: step-by-step movement, who may crawl, option-versus-example classification beyond naming the choice, prop artwork, and any replay the texts never describe.

A failed beat sends the kids back only by that beat’s own rule. Where a beat writes no failure rule, this slice marks UNKNOWN and does not invent one.

## 2. Requirements

### One session

**REQ-01-001.** The play is one session of two phases in order: Phase A is Game 1, and Phase B is Game 2.

> Merge rule. “One play session with two phases.” “Phase A is Game 1, beats 1–5.” “Phase B starts immediately in the same room.”

**REQ-01-002.** The session keeps all ten beats in the written order: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, then 6, 7, 8, 9, 10.

> Merge rule. “Do not drop any of the ten beats.” “beats 1–5, in the order written.” “Beats 6–10 run in the order written.”

**REQ-01-003.** The kids are the ones who try to take the target in Phase A and to deliver the funny secret note in Phase B.

> Game 1. “The kids must sneak up from the back of the room to grab it.” Game 2. “The kids are at the back of the classroom and need to deliver a funny secret note.”

**REQ-01-004.** The session has one teacher, in the room for both phases.

> Game 1. “The teacher is sleeping at their desk.” Game 2. “The teacher is standing facing a whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book.” Merge rule. “The teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher.”

**REQ-01-005.** At least two kids are in the session.

> Game 2 beat 7. “pass a giant, oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line.” Game 2 beat 9. “one kid must yell” and “the other kids can crawl forward.”

### Phase A — who, where, teacher, goal

**REQ-01-006.** Phase A starts with the kids at the back of the room.

> Game 1. “The kids must sneak up from the back of the room.”

**REQ-01-007.** Phase A starts with the teacher sleeping at their desk at the front of the room.

> Game 1. “The teacher is sleeping at their desk at the front of the room.”

**REQ-01-008.** That sleeping teacher is guarding the target.

> Game 1. “guarding the target (a golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats).”

**REQ-01-009.** The guarded target is a golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats.

> Game 1. “a golden trophy, a toy, or a bucket of treats.”

**REQ-01-010.** In Phase A the kids try to sneak from the back of the room to the desk and grab the target.

> Game 1. “The kids must sneak up from the back of the room to grab it.”

**REQ-01-011.** If the teacher wakes up during Phase A, the kids must immediately freeze in a ridiculous, funny pose.

> Game 1. “If the teacher wakes up, the kids must immediately freeze in a ridiculous, funny pose.”

### Phase A beats

**REQ-01-012.** Beat 1, The Squeaky Toy Shoes: the kids walk across the room while holding a squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap under their feet.

> Game 1 beat 1. “Kids must walk across the room while holding a squeaky dog toy or bubble wrap under their feet.”

**REQ-01-013.** On the beat 1 walk, sneaking silently is impossible.

> Game 1 beat 1. “Sneaking silently is impossible.”

**REQ-01-014.** MERGE-CHOICE. Finishing the beat 1 walk is not the prize claim. Beats 2, 3, 4, and 5 still follow in that order. The mid-way checkpoint stays on beat 4, and reaching the desk stays on beat 5.

> Merge rule. Beats 1–5 stay in order, and none of the ten beats is dropped. Game 1 beat 4 still has “a mid-way checkpoint line.” Game 1 beat 5 still has “reaches the desk.” Smallest join: the beat 1 crossing does not replace those later beats.

**REQ-01-015.** Beat 2, The Feather Tickle Trigger: if the teacher starts to wake up, a kid can throw a soft plush toy onto the designated shield mat.

> Game 1 beat 2. “If the teacher starts to wake up, a kid can throw a soft plush toy onto a designated shield mat.”

**REQ-01-016.** A plush toy on that shield mat makes the teacher laugh uncontrollably for 5 seconds and gives everyone that time to move.

> Game 1 beat 2. “instantly force the teacher to start laughing uncontrollably for 5 seconds, giving everyone time to move.”

**REQ-01-017.** MERGE-CHOICE. Beat 2 stays in the written order, and the plush throw still happens only if the teacher starts to wake up. The beat stays in the session. The teacher is not given an extra wake.

> Merge rule. “Do not drop any of the ten beats.” Game 1 beat 2. “If the teacher starts to wake up.” Smallest join: keep the slot and keep the “if.”

**REQ-01-018.** Beat 3, The Copycat Pose: when the teacher suddenly opens their eyes, anyone caught moving must instantly freeze and imitate a goofy statue.

> Game 1 beat 3. “When the teacher suddenly opens their eyes, anyone caught moving must instantly freeze and imitate a goofy statue.”

**REQ-01-019.** If they laugh at that freeze, they have to take three steps back.

> Game 1 beat 3. “If they laugh, they have to take three steps back.”

**REQ-01-020.** Beat 4, The Sticky Note Sabotage: to pass the mid-way checkpoint line, a kid must stick a funny sticky note on the teacher’s back without the teacher noticing.

> Game 1 beat 4. “To pass a mid-way checkpoint line, a kid must successfully sneak up and stick a funny sticky note … onto the teacher’s back without the teacher noticing.”

**REQ-01-021.** Beat 5, The Confetti Victory Blast: a kid who reaches the desk without getting caught claims the target prize.

> Game 1 beat 5. “When a kid successfully reaches the desk without getting caught … as they claim the target prize.”

**REQ-01-022.** MERGE-CHOICE. That claim’s victory blast is popping a safe party popper or throwing a handful of paper confetti over the teacher’s head. Either one is the confetti victory blast that can end Phase A.

> Game 1 beat 5. “pop a safe party popper or throw a handful of paper confetti over the teacher's head.” Merge rule. “the confetti victory blast.” Smallest join: the party popper stays a form of that blast.

**REQ-01-023.** Phase A ends when a kid claims the prize with the victory blast.

> Merge rule. “It ends when a kid claims the prize with the confetti victory blast.”

### Phase B begins

**REQ-01-024.** Phase B starts immediately when Phase A ends.

> Merge rule. “Phase B starts immediately in the same room.”

**REQ-01-025.** Phase B is that same room, and props from Phase A are still in the room.

> Merge rule. “in the same room.” “Props and the room carry from Phase A into Phase B.”

**REQ-01-026.** As Phase B starts, the teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher.

> Merge rule. “The teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher.”

**REQ-01-027.** The strict teacher pretends to be very strict and is standing facing a whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book.

> Game 2. “standing facing a whiteboard, typing on a computer, or reading a big book, pretending to be very strict.”

**REQ-01-028.** MERGE-CHOICE. Phase B starts with the kids at the back of the classroom. This includes the moment just after a kid claimed the prize at the front desk. The join is that start position. The games do not describe a walk back from the desk.

> Game 2. “The kids are at the back of the classroom.” Merge rule. Phase B “starts immediately in the same room.” Smallest join that keeps both: kids are at the back when Phase B begins.

**REQ-01-029.** In Phase B the kids try to deliver a funny secret note to a target mailbox on the other side of the room.

> Game 2. “deliver a funny secret note to a target mailbox on the other side of the room.”

### Phase B beats

**REQ-01-030.** Beat 6, The Whoopee Cushion Pathway: the kids guess which cushions are safe to step on. The floor is lined with cushions, and three of them hide whoopee cushions.

> Game 2 beat 6. “The floor is lined with cushions, but three of them have hidden whoopee cushions underneath. The kids have to guess which ones are safe to step on.”

**REQ-01-031.** Stepping on a hidden whoopee cushion makes a loud, funny noise.

> Game 2 beat 6. “resulting in loud, funny noises if they pick wrong.”

**REQ-01-032.** Beat 7, The Reverse Homework Trick: the kids pass a giant, oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line using only their feet, without dropping them.

> Game 2 beat 7. “kids must pass a giant, oversized pair of clown sunglasses down the line using only their feet, without dropping them.”

**REQ-01-033.** Beat 8, The Doodle Attack: when the teacher turns around to face the board, the kids race to draw a funny hat or giant ears on the teacher’s shadow on the wall with a flashlight.

> Game 2 beat 8. “When the teacher turns around to face the board, kids must quickly race to draw a funny hat or giant ears on the teacher's shadow on the wall using a flashlight.”

**REQ-01-034.** MERGE-CHOICE. Beat 8 stays in the written order, and the race still waits until the teacher turns around to face the board. The beat stays in the session. No different signal replaces that turn.

> Merge rule. “Do not drop any of the ten beats.” Game 2 beat 8. “When the teacher turns around to face the board.” Smallest join: keep the slot and keep the “when.”

**REQ-01-035.** Beat 9, The Monster Talk Distraction: to move forward, one kid yells a silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice.

> Game 2 beat 9. “To move forward, one kid must yell out a silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice.”

**REQ-01-036.** While the teacher turns around, confused, to investigate that sound, the other kids can crawl forward.

> Game 2 beat 9. “While the teacher turns around confused to investigate the sound, the other kids can crawl forward.”

**REQ-01-037.** Beat 10, The Silly String Finale: once the secret note reaches the target mailbox, the kids gently spray the teacher’s desk or the teacher’s shoes with silly string and do a victory dance.

> Game 2 beat 10. “Once the secret note reaches the target mailbox, the kids win the game and get to gently spray the teacher's desk (or the teacher's shoes) with silly string while doing a victory dance.”

**REQ-01-038.** Phase B ends with the silly string finale.

> Merge rule. “It ends with the silly string finale.”

### Session win and failure

**REQ-01-039.** MERGE-CHOICE. The silly string finale wins Phase B. The session is won only when that finale has happened and a kid has already claimed the prize with the Phase A victory blast.

> Game 2 beat 10. “the kids win the game.” Merge rule. “The session is won only when both finales have happened.” Smallest join: beat 10’s win is the Phase B win; the session win is both finales, Phase A then Phase B.

**REQ-01-040.** When a beat fails, the kids are sent back only by the rule written for that beat.

> Merge rule. “A failed beat sends the kids back as that beat’s own rule says.”

**REQ-01-041.** The three steps back after a laugh on the beat 3 freeze are the only send-back either game writes.

> Game 1 beat 3. “If they laugh, they have to take three steps back.” Merge rule. If a beat has no failure rule, do not invent one.

## 4. Unknowns

- The exact number of kids, above the minimum of two. Which kid must finish a beat that says “a kid” before the next beat starts. Phase A does end when one kid claims the prize with the blast; whether every other kid must already have finished beats 1–4 is UNKNOWN.
- Where beat 1’s “across the room” ends. REQ-01-014 keeps beats 2–5, and the source never says how far the squeaky walk goes before beat 2.
- How beat 2 is left if the teacher never starts to wake, and how beat 8 is left if the teacher never turns to face the board. Both beats stay in the order and keep their conditions (REQ-01-017, REQ-01-034).
- What completes beats 6, 7, and 9 so the next beat may start. Beat 6 states a wrong-step noise and no finish line. Beat 7 states a feet-only pass without a drop and no finish line. Beat 9 states the yell and the crawl and no finish line.
- Send-back is unstated for beats 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10. That includes the teacher noticing the sticky note, getting caught before the desk, a whoopee noise beyond the noise itself, dropping the sunglasses, drawing at the wrong time, and a yell that does not match the written voice. Beat 6’s noise is the written result of a wrong step, and it is not written as a return.
- Who “they” are in the three-step laugh penalty, how long a step is, and which way “back” faces. What that kid does after the three steps is UNKNOWN. The steps are not a replay.
- Whether “wakes up,” “starts to wake up,” and “suddenly opens their eyes” are one moment or three. Both the Phase A wake freeze and the beat 3 statue stay written.
- Whether any Phase A rule, including the wake freeze and the squeaky-shoe walk, is still in force after its beat or after the teacher stands up. Props and the room do carry. Phase B’s own text gives the strict teacher, the turn to the board, and the confused investigation.
- How the kids come to be at the back when Phase B starts immediately. REQ-01-028 sets that position and does not name a trip from the desk.
- When the Phase B objects are placed: cushions, the three whoopee cushions, mailbox, secret note, sunglasses, flashlight, and silly string. The phase starts immediately, and no setup beat is written.
- Where the mailbox, the mid-way line, the shield mat, the whiteboard, and the desk sit relative to one another. Whether “the board” in beat 8 is the whiteboard named for the strict teacher is not spelled out past those words.
- Where the claimed prize is after the claim, and whether the sticky note is still on the teacher’s back in Phase B.
- Who picks each named alternative, and when that pick is fixed. Whether the strict teacher’s activity changes during Phase B. Whether one claim may use both the party popper and the paper confetti, and whether one finale may spray both the desk and the shoes.
- Replay of a beat, a phase, or the session. Clearing a choice and picking again. No text describes either.
- Whether the teacher has a separate win. The written wins are the kids’ Phase B win at the silly string finale and the session win when both finales have happened.
- Who carries the secret note, who sprays the silly string, and whether every kid dances.

## 5. Handoff

- Slice 04 owns step, freeze, crawl, and the movement reading of the three-step send-back. This slice keeps the session order, the phase boundary, and the rule that only a beat’s own text may send kids back.
- Slice 08 owns which “or” is a closed choice, which “like” is only a sample, and the finding that replay is unwritten. This slice names those options where the loop uses them and does not define replay.
- Beat 2: who designates the shield mat, and what “onto” the mat requires. Here, the conditional throw, the 5-second laugh, and that the beat stays in order.
- Beat 4: mustache and goofy-face samples. Here, the unnoticed note is what passes the mid-way line.
- Beat 5: what “caught” means. Here, the desk claim plus the victory blast is what ends Phase A.
- Beat 6: which cushions hide the whoopee cushions. Here, the guess, the wrong-step noise, and that the noise is not a written send-back.
- Beat 7: who stands in the line. Here, the feet-only pass without a drop.
- Beat 8: what counts as the hat or the ears. Here, the race when the teacher faces the board, and that the beat stays conditional.
- Beat 9: how the monster voice is performed beyond a silly phrase in a deep, goofy monster voice. Here, one kid yells and the other kids may crawl.
- Beat 10: who sprays, and what “gently” requires. Here, the mailbox arrival, the desk-or-shoes spray, the victory dance, and the session win.
- Props and space: distances, which wall, and which side “the other side” is, beyond the front desk, the back of the room, the mid-way line, and the mailbox on the other side.
