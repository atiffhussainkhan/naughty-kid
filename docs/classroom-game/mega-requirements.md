# Mega requirements — combined classroom game

The one document a designer reads. Edited from the brief and slices 01–10. The brief wins where a slice disagrees with it. Those settled disagreements are in section 10. Four contradictions the brief does not settle are DESIGN-BLOCKERs in section 7. They are not requirements, and this document does not pick a side.

Nothing here adds a mechanic, prop, timer, score, distance, player count, or safety rule that the brief or the slices do not already contain.

## 1. How to read this

One session, one room, one teacher. Phase A is Game 1, beats 1–5, in that order. It ends when a kid claims the target prize with the confetti victory blast. Phase B starts immediately in that same room. The teacher stands up and becomes the strict teacher. Beats 6–10 run in that order and end with the silly string finale. The session is won only when both finales have happened. Props and the room carry from Phase A into Phase B. A beat with no failure rule stays UNKNOWN.

The requirements in section 3 are the testable statements. This opening is only the map. 76 requirements. Ids run REQ-MEGA-001 through REQ-MEGA-077, except REQ-MEGA-037, which is retired. The id map is in `mega-review.md`.

Each requirement is one statement. A real choice is met by any one option in its “or” list. A “like” sample is an example, not the test. Source tone is not a pass/fail test. UNKNOWN is left open. Do not fill a number, a position, a timer, a player count, or a failure result.

## 7. DESIGN-BLOCKERs

Four open contradictions. The brief does not decide them. Do not build past them by picking a side.

**DB-1. Wake-up, starting to wake, and eyes open.**

- Statement A: If the teacher wakes up, the kids immediately freeze in a pose (REQ-MEGA-007). Source: Game 1 opening.
- Statement B: If the teacher starts to wake up, a kid can throw a soft plush toy, and the teacher then laughs for 5 seconds (REQ-MEGA-010, REQ-MEGA-011). Source: Game 1 beat 2.
- Statement C: When the teacher suddenly opens their eyes, anyone caught moving instantly freezes and imitates a statue (REQ-MEGA-012, REQ-MEGA-050). Source: Game 1 beat 3.

The brief uses three phrases. It does not say they are one moment, and it does not say they are three moments. Both the wake-up sentence and the eyes-open sentence stay (REQ-MEGA-015).

A designer would wrongly assume either that one moment has to satisfy the pose, the plush, and the statue together, or that the session must schedule three different teacher events.

**DB-2. Move during the 5-second laugh, and freeze if the teacher wakes up.**

- Statement A: Those 5 seconds give everyone time to move (REQ-MEGA-049). Source: Game 1 beat 2.
- Statement B: If the teacher wakes up, the kids immediately freeze (REQ-MEGA-007). Source: Game 1 opening.

The brief does not say the laugh suspends the freeze, and it does not say the freeze suspends the 5 seconds of movement. It does not say “starts to wake up” is a different moment from “wakes up.”

A designer would wrongly assume either that the laugh cancels the freeze, or that the freeze cancels the time to move.

**DB-3. Whiteboard, and the board.**

- Statement A: The strict teacher may be standing facing a whiteboard (REQ-MEGA-057). Source: Game 2 opening.
- Statement B: The doodle happens when the teacher turns around to face the board (REQ-MEGA-030). Source: Game 2 beat 8.

The brief does not say “the board” is the whiteboard. It does not say the teacher turns away from the whiteboard before beat 8. It does not add a second board.

A designer would wrongly assume either that beat 8 turns the teacher toward the whiteboard the strict-teacher activity may already be using, or that the room contains a second board.

**DB-4. “Win the game,” and win the session.**

- Statement A: Once the secret note reaches the target mailbox, the kids win the game. Source: Game 2 beat 10. Slice lines: REQ-06-036, REQ-10-038, and the win clauses of REQ-01-039 and REQ-06-037.
- Statement B: The session is won only when both finales have happened (REQ-MEGA-036). Source: Merge rule.

Applied from the brief: statement B stays. Beat 10’s sentence also stays. The written order puts the confetti victory blast before beat 10. The brief does not say whether “the game” means the session.

A designer would wrongly assume either that the mailbox sentence wins the session even when the confetti victory blast has not happened, or that the kids receive a Game 2 win and a separate session win.
